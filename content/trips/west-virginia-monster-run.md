West Virginia has more of this per mile than anywhere else in the country, and
the reason is not folklore. It is that the state built a great many large public
institutions in the nineteenth century, ran out of money to knock them down, and
the buildings are still standing.

This route runs roughly north to south down US-250 and US-19, then west to the
Ohio River. It is about three hundred miles of driving and the roads are slow —
that is the point, but budget for it.

## How to pace it

**Day one** is the two big buildings, and they are heavy going. Moundsville and
Weston are ninety minutes apart and each takes two hours properly. Do not try to
add anything else.

**Day two** is the relief: Sutton and Ansted, a free museum and a tilted shack,
both of them run by people who are glad you came.

**Day three** is Point Pleasant, which needs a morning rather than a day unless
you have timed it for the September festival.

## One thing worth knowing

The order here is deliberate. Ending at Point Pleasant means finishing with the
museum and the statue and then the bridge memorial, four minutes apart, in that
order — which is the only sequence that makes sense of the place.

Doing it in reverse works geographically and lands badly.
