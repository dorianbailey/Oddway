OddWay — Missouri Complete
127 Missouri stops
Stops 1–127
Prepared September 9, 2026

Access key: open = normally accessible during posted/public hours; limited = seasonal, scheduled, ticketed, cemetery-hours, appointment-only, or check-ahead access; roadside = view from a safe lawful public area; private = private property, do not enter without permission; seasonal = primarily accessible during a defined season; view-only = observe from a lawful public viewpoint and do not enter the feature.

Research note: Round Spring Cave is intentionally excluded because the National Park Service lists it closed for the entire 2026 season. Bonne Terre Mine is also excluded because its operator currently lists the mine as closed. Round Spring itself remains included as a public surface stop.

1. Missouri State Penitentiary Tours
Jefferson City, MO
38.57350, -92.16070
dark-history | limited
Opened in 1836 and closed in 2004, the Missouri State Penitentiary preserves cell blocks, yards and the former gas chamber from one of the country's oldest major prisons west of the Mississippi. Scheduled history tours and paranormal investigations make the enormous complex a particularly strong OddWay stop.
Source: https://missouripentours.com/

2. Missouri State Penitentiary Museum
Jefferson City, MO
38.57204, -92.16358
dark-history | limited
This museum complements the nearby prison complex with photographs, artifacts, inmate-made objects and displays interpreting daily life inside the Missouri State Penitentiary. It gives travelers a lower-intensity way to explore the prison's history before or after a scheduled tour.
Source: https://missouripentours.com/museum/

3. Lemp Mansion
St. Louis, MO
38.59317, -90.21581
paranormal | limited
The mansion of St. Louis's once-powerful Lemp brewing family is tied to a series of deaths and suicides that helped create one of the city's best-known haunted-house legends. The restored property operates as an inn and event venue and periodically offers ghost-focused experiences.
Source: https://www.lempmansion.com/

4. Pythian Castle
Springfield, MO
37.22143, -93.26868
paranormal | limited
Built in the early twentieth century for the Knights of Pythias and later used by the U.S. military, this fortress-like building has accumulated prison, military and paranormal lore. Public access is through scheduled tours, events and selected paranormal programs rather than unrestricted walk-in exploration.
Source: https://www.pythiancastle.com/

5. The Elms Hotel & Spa Paranormal Tour
Excelsior Springs, MO
39.33686, -94.22600
paranormal | limited
The historic Elms has survived fires, Prohibition-era intrigue and decades as a mineral-water resort, giving the hotel a deep supply of stories about unusual activity. The property has offered scheduled paranormal tours that explore its darker legends and hidden spaces.
Source: https://www.hyatt.com/destination-by-hyatt/en-US/mkcdc-the-elms-hotel-and-spa

6. Beattie Mansion
St. Joseph, MO
39.77500, -94.85980
paranormal | limited
This 1850s mansion is one of St. Joseph's best-known paranormal investigation locations. The privately operated property offers scheduled tours and extended investigations, so OddWay travelers should reserve rather than simply arriving and entering.
Source: https://www.thebeattiemansion.com/

7. Vaile Victorian Mansion
Independence, MO
39.10758, -94.41713
weird-history | limited
Built in 1881, the ornate Second Empire mansion contains elaborate interiors, unusual architectural details and stories surrounding the Vaile family. Seasonal tours make it a strong combination of Gilded Age excess, local tragedy and Victorian atmosphere.
Source: https://vailemansion.org/

8. Glore Psychiatric Museum
St. Joseph, MO
39.77639, -94.80839
medical-history | limited
Housed in a former state hospital building, the Glore traces the history of psychiatric treatment through reconstructed devices, patient art, medical equipment and institutional artifacts. Its displays range from early treatment methods to more modern mental-health history and are unusually specific rather than generic museum material.
Source: https://www.stjosephmuseum.org/glore-psychiatric-museum

9. Haunted Castle House
Brumley, MO
38.09046, -92.48272
paranormal | limited
The turreted Castle House has been used as a residence and medical property and is promoted around its long-running paranormal reputation. Tours and investigations are reservation-based, so it should be treated as a scheduled private attraction rather than a walk-up abandoned site.
Source: https://hauntedcastlehouse.com/

10. Emmaus Asylum
Marthasville, MO
38.64380, -90.99267
paranormal | limited
This historic property is operated as a dedicated paranormal investigation venue with overnight and scheduled access. The site gives OddWay a legitimate routeable haunted-location stop without encouraging entry into an abandoned or unauthorized structure.
Source: https://www.emmausasylum.com/

11. Ozark Spooklight
Joplin, MO
37.08445, -94.51345
folklore | roadside
The Ozark or Hornet Spooklight is a long-running borderland legend involving a mysterious light reported along rural roads southwest of Joplin. Modern explanations point toward distant vehicle headlights, but the story remains one of Missouri's classic supernatural roadside traditions; stay on public roads and never enter adjacent private fields.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/ozark-spooklight

12. Jim the Wonder Dog Memorial Garden
Marshall, MO
39.12157, -93.19733
folklore | open
Jim was a Llewellin setter celebrated in the 1930s for apparently understanding complex spoken commands, predicting events and identifying objects he had not been trained to recognize. A public memorial garden in downtown Marshall preserves the story of the dog whose abilities became local legend.
Source: http://www.jimthewonderdog.org/

13. Jesse James Home Museum
St. Joseph, MO
39.75580, -94.84510
crime-history | limited
Jesse James was shot and killed in this St. Joseph house in 1882 by Robert Ford. The preserved home contains artifacts and interpretation focused on James's final days, making it one of Missouri's most concrete locations tied to the outlaw's life and death.
Source: https://stjosephmuseum.org/jesse-james-home/

14. Jesse James Birthplace & Farm Museum
Kearney, MO
39.39222, -94.32052
crime-history | limited
The James family farm preserves the birthplace and childhood setting of outlaw Jesse James. Museum exhibits and the historic house place the famous robbery stories within the larger history of the James family, slavery, guerrilla warfare and post-Civil War Missouri.
Source: https://jessejamesmuseum.org/

15. Jesse James Bank Museum
Liberty, MO
39.24697, -94.41926
crime-history | limited
The former Clay County Savings Association building was the target of an 1866 daylight robbery often associated with the James-Younger gang, although the exact identities of the robbers remain debated. The museum preserves the bank interior and the story of one of the earliest major postwar outlaw raids.
Source: https://jessejamesmuseum.org/jesse-james-bank-museum/

16. Jesse James Grave
Kearney, MO
39.36747, -94.36426
crime-history | limited
Jesse James's remains were ultimately reinterred in Mount Olivet Cemetery beneath a family marker after first being buried at the James farm. The grave is a routeable endpoint to the outlaw's story and should be visited respectfully during lawful cemetery hours.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/jesse-james-grave

17. Odd Fellows Home / Belvoir Winery
Liberty, MO
39.22581, -94.40661
paranormal | limited
The former Odd Fellows complex included an orphanage, hospital, nursing home and cemetery, creating a large institutional landscape with a substantial paranormal reputation. The property is privately operated; public access is limited to business hours, events and authorized ghost tours rather than entry into closed buildings.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/odd-fellows-home

18. Lemp Family Tomb
St. Louis, MO
38.68973, -90.22461
cemetery-oddities | limited
The Lemp family mausoleum in Bellefontaine Cemetery holds members of the brewing dynasty whose tragedies helped fuel the haunted reputation of the Lemp Mansion. It is a useful companion stop to the mansion and can be visited respectfully under cemetery rules.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/the-lemp-family-tomb

19. Old St. Ferdinand Shrine
Florissant, MO
38.79639, -90.33389
religious-history | limited
This historic Catholic complex preserves an 1819 convent, 1821 church and other early buildings connected with St. Rose Philippine Duchesne. Religious artifacts, frontier history and the unusually old surviving structures make it a distinctive stop in suburban St. Louis.
Source: http://www.oldstferdinandshrine.com/

20. Queen Tombstone
Charleston, MO
36.94011, -89.35138
cemetery-oddities | limited
A striking grave marker in the Charleston area depicts a crowned woman and has generated local curiosity far beyond an ordinary headstone. The stop is valuable as cemetery folk art, but visitors should stay on established paths and treat the burial ground respectfully.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/grave-of-the-queen

21. Grave of David Rice Atchison
Plattsburg, MO
39.55818, -94.44455
weird-history | limited
Senator David Rice Atchison is buried beneath a monument that repeats the long-running claim that he was 'president for one day' during the 1849 presidential transition. Historians do not regard him as an actual U.S. president, but the story remains one of Missouri's most durable political-history oddities.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/david-rice-atchison-grave

22. Far West Temple Site
Kingston, MO
39.67160, -94.13070
religious-history | open
Far West was an important Latter-day Saint settlement in the 1830s before conflict forced its residents from the area. Cornerstones from the planned temple remain on the preserved site, creating a quiet but highly specific religious-history stop.
Source: https://history.churchofjesuschrist.org/subsection/historic-sites/missouri/far-west?lang=eng

23. Burial Place of Chief Pontiac
St. Louis, MO
38.62456, -90.19037
weird-history | roadside
A plaque near the former colonial village area marks the traditional St. Louis burial place of Odawa leader Pontiac, who was killed in 1769. The exact burial history is not perfectly documented, but the marker anchors an important and often-overlooked part of regional Native and colonial history.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/burial-place-of-chief-pontiac

24. Marconnott Mausoleum / Mount Olive Mummy
St. Louis, MO
38.53439, -90.29188
cemetery-oddities | limited
The Marconnott mausoleum became locally famous because a naturally mummified body was once visible through a glass panel. The coffin is no longer displayed for public viewing, but the mausoleum and story remain part of Mount Olive Cemetery's unusual history; visit respectfully.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/mount-olive-mummy

25. Meramec Caverns
Stanton, MO
38.24155, -91.09224
caves | limited
Meramec Caverns is a large commercial cave system beneath the Ozark hills that has drawn Route 66 travelers for generations. Guided tours pass through extensive limestone rooms and formations, with the attraction also trading heavily on regional Jesse James lore.
Source: https://www.americascave.com/

26. Fantastic Caverns
Springfield, MO
37.28800, -93.35962
caves | limited
Fantastic Caverns is toured in passenger vehicles rather than on foot, making it one of the few ride-through show caves in the United States. Visitors travel through large passages filled with flowstone, columns and other formations on scheduled guided tours.
Source: https://fantasticcaverns.com/

27. Mark Twain Cave
Hannibal, MO
39.68875, -91.33166
caves | limited
Known to Samuel Clemens as a boy and later woven into The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Mark Twain Cave is Missouri's oldest continuously operating show cave. Guided tours explore its maze-like passages while connecting the geology directly to Hannibal's literary history.
Source: https://www.marktwaincave.com/

28. Cameron Cave
Hannibal, MO
39.68851, -91.33175
caves | seasonal
Cameron Cave is the less-developed companion to Mark Twain Cave and is toured by lantern rather than permanent electric lighting. Its narrow maze-like passages provide a noticeably different experience from the neighboring commercial tour route.
Source: https://www.marktwaincave.com/

29. Onondaga Cave
Leasburg, MO
38.06120, -91.22718
caves | seasonal
Onondaga Cave contains a dense concentration of stalactites, stalagmites, flowstone and large rooms beneath its namesake state park. Missouri State Parks offers scheduled guided tours during the 2026 cave season.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/key-location/onondaga-cave

30. Cathedral Cave
Leasburg, MO
38.05644, -91.24099
caves | seasonal
Cathedral Cave is a separate cave system inside Onondaga Cave State Park, reached on a more limited guided-tour schedule. The route includes undeveloped passages and the large formation known as the Cathedral Bell.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/page/onondaga-cave-state-park/cathedral-cave

31. Fisher Cave
Sullivan, MO
38.22731, -91.08430
caves | seasonal
Fisher Cave lies beneath Meramec State Park and is opened for naturalist-led tours during the park's cave season. The tour follows an active cave environment with streams, wildlife and varied limestone formations rather than a heavily commercialized interior.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/page/meramec-state-park/fisher-cave

32. Ozark Caverns
Kaiser, MO
38.05459, -92.58354
caves | seasonal
Ozark Caverns is an undeveloped cave at Lake of the Ozarks State Park where tours rely on hand-held lanterns instead of installed electric lighting. Angel Showers, a formation created by water falling from the cave ceiling, is one of its signature features.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/page/lake-ozarks-state-park/ozark-caverns

33. Bridal Cave
Camdenton, MO
38.02353, -92.79193
caves | limited
Bridal Cave is a long-running Lake of the Ozarks show cave known for dramatic formations and an underground chapel where weddings are performed. Guided tours make it an easy routeable cave stop rather than an adventure-caving location.
Source: https://bridalcave.com/

34. Jacob's Cave
Versailles, MO
38.33529, -92.83582
caves | limited
Jacob's Cave offers guided walking tours through a large Ozark cavern filled with reflective pools, columns and other formations. The cave has operated as a tourist attraction for decades and remains a dependable central-Missouri subterranean stop.
Source: http://www.jacobscave.com/

35. Talking Rocks Cavern
Branson West, MO
36.67727, -93.37743
caves | limited
Talking Rocks Cavern descends through a tightly packed collection of cave formations near Branson West. Guided tours use stairs and walkways to reach richly decorated rooms that feel very different from Missouri's larger horizontal cave systems.
Source: https://talkingrockscavern.com/

36. Smallin Civil War Cave
Ozark, MO
37.05123, -93.18681
caves | limited
Smallin Cave has a massive entrance and a human history tied to Native use, settlement and Civil War-era stories. Guided tours combine geology with regional history, and seasonal lantern programs go deeper into the cave's folklore.
Source: https://smallincave.com/

37. Bluff Dwellers Cave
Noel, MO
36.51883, -94.48699
caves | limited
Discovered in the 1920s and opened as a show cave soon afterward, Bluff Dwellers Cave preserves extensive speleothems and archaeological context from the Ozarks. Guided tours operate from the privately run cave complex outside Noel.
Source: https://bluffdwellerscave.com/

38. Lost Canyon Cave & Nature Trail
Ridgedale, MO
36.53713, -93.24746
caves | limited
Lost Canyon is explored by self-driven golf cart along a constructed nature trail that passes waterfalls, rock formations and a cave containing an underground bar. It is highly developed, but the unusual cave-drive format earns it a place in the OddWay database.
Source: https://topoftherock.com/top-of-the-rock/

39. Marvel Cave
Branson, MO
36.66708, -93.33842
caves | limited
Marvel Cave was the original attraction around which Silver Dollar City developed and contains one of the largest cave entrance rooms in North America. Theme-park admission includes scheduled cave tours that descend hundreds of feet below the surface before returning visitors by cable train.
Source: https://www.silverdollarcity.com/theme-park/attractions/rides/marvel-cave/

40. Devil's Icebox Entrance
Columbia, MO
38.87089, -92.32663
caves | open
A boardwalk in Rock Bridge Memorial State Park leads to the dramatic sinkhole and cave entrance known as the Devil's Icebox. Wild-cave tours are suspended, so OddWay should route visitors only to the lawful surface trail and entrance-viewing area.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/key-location/devils-icebox-entrance

41. Ha Ha Tonka Castle Ruins
Camdenton, MO
37.97608, -92.76985
ruins | open
The stone ruins of an early twentieth-century mansion crown a bluff above the Lake of the Ozarks after the building was destroyed by fire in 1942. Public trails and overlooks let travelers explore the stabilized exterior ruins without trespassing.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/key-location/castle-ruins

42. Ha Ha Tonka Natural Bridge
Camdenton, MO
37.97446, -92.76325
geology | open
This massive natural limestone bridge is one of several karst features concentrated inside Ha Ha Tonka State Park. A maintained trail reaches the arch and makes it an easy addition to the nearby castle ruins and spring.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/ha-ha-tonka-state-park

43. Ha Ha Tonka Colosseum
Camdenton, MO
37.97482, -92.76312
geology | open
The Colosseum is a huge collapsed sinkhole, roughly 150 feet deep, created by the same karst processes that shaped Ha Ha Tonka's caves and natural bridge. The feature is reached from a signed state-park trail rather than by entering the collapse itself.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/ha-ha-tonka-state-park

44. Ha Ha Tonka Spring
Camdenton, MO
37.97388, -92.76720
springs | open
A large blue spring emerges at the base of the Ha Ha Tonka bluffs and feeds a clear stream toward the lake. Boardwalks and trails connect the spring with the park's other geological features and historic ruins.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/ha-ha-tonka-state-park

45. Elephant Rocks State Park
Belleview, MO
37.65179, -90.68980
geology | open
Enormous rounded granite boulders stand in rows and clusters that resemble a train of circus elephants. A paved loop trail threads among the 1.5-billion-year-old rocks and past traces of the area's quarrying history.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/elephant-rocks-state-park

46. Engine House Ruin
Graniteville, MO
37.65586, -90.68472
industrial-ruins | open
Stone walls from a nineteenth-century railroad engine house survive among the granite landscape near Elephant Rocks. The ruin is a compact industrial-history counterpart to the park's better-known natural formations.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/key-location/engine-house-ruins

47. Johnson's Shut-Ins
Middle Brook, MO
37.53913, -90.83806
geology | open
The East Fork of the Black River is squeezed through resistant volcanic rock here, creating chutes, potholes and cascades known as shut-ins. Designated public access makes this one of Missouri's most dramatic routeable geological stops.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/johnsons-shut-ins-state-park

48. Taum Sauk Mountain High Point
Ironton, MO
37.57119, -90.72932
geology | open
A short trail leads to the highest natural point in Missouri at Taum Sauk Mountain. The high point itself is subtle, but the surrounding St. Francois Mountains are among the oldest exposed mountain rocks in North America.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/key-location/high-point

49. Mina Sauk Falls
Ironton, MO
37.56250, -90.74086
waterfalls | open
Mina Sauk Falls drops in a series of cascades down Taum Sauk Mountain and is generally considered Missouri's tallest waterfall. Its flow is seasonal, so the rugged trail is most rewarding after wet weather.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/taum-sauk-mountain-state-park

50. The Pinnacles
Sturgeon, MO
39.13476, -92.32347
geology | open
A narrow limestone ridge rises sharply between two creeks at this Boone County park, creating fins, cracks and elevated scrambling routes unusual for central Missouri. Visitors should stay on established public access and use caution on exposed rock.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/the-pinnacles

51. Bennett Spring Natural Tunnel
Lebanon, MO
37.70383, -92.84013
geology | open
A natural rock tunnel along a Bennett Spring State Park trail creates a compact cave-like passage through the Ozark limestone. It is a good surface-geology stop for travelers who do not want a scheduled commercial cave tour.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/bennett-spring-natural-tunnel

52. Devil's Honeycomb
Ste. Genevieve, MO
37.81069, -90.71542
geology | open
Wind, water and erosion have carved a sandstone wall into a honeycomb of cavities along a trail in the Hawn State Park area. The texture is striking at close range and fits OddWay's preference for routeable natural features with a clear physical object to find.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/devils-honeycomb

53. Portuguese Point
Montreal, MO
37.90726, -92.09794
geology | open
Portuguese Point is an exposed Ozark overlook with dramatic rock shelves above the surrounding river-and-lake country. Its unusual name and rugged stone viewpoint make it a worthwhile short geological detour when conditions are safe.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/portuguese-point

54. Greer Spring
Alton, MO
36.78661, -91.34753
springs | open
Greer Spring is one of the largest springs in the Ozarks, releasing an enormous volume of clear water that quickly forms a substantial stream. A roughly one-mile trail descends from the public parking area to the spring branch.
Source: https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/places/greer-spring

55. Roaring River Spring
Cassville, MO
36.59131, -93.83241
springs | open
A deep blue spring emerges from a cavernous opening beneath a cliff and forms the headwaters of Roaring River. The spring is immediately accessible inside the state park and pairs natural geology with the park's historic trout-hatchery landscape.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/roaring-river-state-park

56. Montauk Springs
Salem, MO
37.46032, -91.68320
springs | open
Multiple large springs rise inside Montauk State Park and combine to form the headwaters of the Current River. Trails and historic mill features make the spring area a strong natural-history stop rather than simply a fishing destination.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/montauk-state-park

57. Alley Spring & Mill
Eminence, MO
37.15394, -91.44430
mills | open
A vivid blue spring flows beside the red Alley Mill, one of the most recognizable historic structures in the Ozark National Scenic Riverways. The combination of karst hydrology and preserved milling machinery makes this a high-value OddWay stop.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/ozar/learn/historyculture/alley-mill.htm

58. Big Spring
Van Buren, MO
36.95304, -90.99310
springs | open
Big Spring pours hundreds of millions of gallons of water into the Current River system and is among the largest springs in the United States. Public trails and overlooks allow travelers to see the immense blue-green discharge safely from the riverways park.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/ozar/learn/nature/big-spring.htm

59. Blue Spring
Eminence, MO
37.16687, -91.16437
springs | open
Blue Spring is famous for its intense sapphire color and exceptional depth. A foot trail from the conservation-area access leads to the spring, which is protected from swimming and other disturbance.
Source: https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/places/blue-spring-natural-area

60. Welch Spring Hospital Ruins
Akers, MO
37.39459, -91.57628
ruins | open
Ruins of a stone hospital stand beside Welch Spring, where an early twentieth-century doctor promoted cave air as a treatment for respiratory illness. The failed medical venture left an unusually photogenic wall directly beside a major Ozark spring.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/welch-spring.htm

61. Rocky Falls
Eminence, MO
37.09490, -91.21018
waterfalls | open
Rocky Falls spills over billion-year-old rhyolite in a broad stair-step cascade. The National Scenic Riverways site is reached by road and short foot access, making the unusual volcanic geology especially easy to visit.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/rocky-falls.htm

62. Grand Gulf State Park
Thayer, MO
36.54415, -91.64499
geology | open
Grand Gulf is a collapsed cave system often described as Missouri's 'Little Grand Canyon.' Visitors can view the deep chasm from rim trails and, when conditions allow, descend designated routes into portions of the natural feature.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/grand-gulf-state-park

63. Graham Cave
Danville, MO
38.90556, -91.57528
archaeology | open
Excavations at Graham Cave revealed evidence of human occupation stretching back thousands of years. A public trail leads to the broad sandstone shelter, while interpretation explains the archaeological importance of the site without encouraging disturbance.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/graham-cave-state-park

64. Pickle Springs Natural Area
Ste. Genevieve, MO
37.80170, -90.30150
geology | open
Pickle Springs protects an extraordinary concentration of sandstone arches, slot-like passages, hoodoos and weathered rock forms. The Trail Through Time loops through the natural area and gives OddWay several strange formations within one routeable stop.
Source: https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/places/pickle-springs-natural-area

65. Castor River Shut-Ins
Fredericktown, MO
37.56882, -90.15506
geology | open
The Castor River squeezes through Missouri's only known pink-granite shut-ins, creating cascades over exposed 1.5-billion-year-old rock. Missouri Department of Conservation provides a public trailhead and a one-mile loop to the formation.
Source: https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/places/natural-areas/castor-river-shut-ins

66. Edward 'Ted' and Pat Jones-Confluence Point
West Alton, MO
38.81607, -90.11953
geography | open
A short trail reaches the point where the Missouri River meets the Mississippi, allowing visitors to stand at one of the most consequential river junctions in North America. Seasonal flooding can affect access, so current state-park conditions should be checked before routing.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/edward-ted-and-pat-jones-confluence-point-state-park

67. Wild Horses of Shannon County
Eminence, MO
37.17214, -91.30052
folklore-nature | open
Several small herds of free-roaming horses live on public lands around the Current and Jacks Fork rivers and have become a distinctive part of Shannon County folklore. Sightings are not guaranteed; travelers should observe from a distance and never feed, chase or block the horses.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/ozar/learn/nature/wild-horses.htm

68. Round Spring
Eminence, MO
37.28255, -91.40764
springs | open
Round Spring rises in a nearly circular blue basin and flows directly toward the Current River. The spring itself remains a valid public stop even though Round Spring Cave is closed for the 2026 season, so OddWay should route only to the spring area.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/round-spring.htm

69. Klepzig Mill
Eminence, MO
37.12650, -91.19852
mills | open
Klepzig Mill is a small historic turbine mill beside Rocky Creek in the Ozark National Scenic Riverways. The remote structure, shut-ins and creek setting create a strong backroad OddWay stop, but access roads can be rough after bad weather.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/klepzig-mill.htm

70. Hodgson Water Mill
Sycamore, MO
36.70971, -92.26641
mills | roadside
Hodgson Mill stands dramatically at the base of a bluff beside a powerful spring that once drove milling machinery. The privately owned building is best treated as a roadside exterior/photo stop unless current access information specifically permits more.
Source: https://www.explorewestplains.com/hodgson-mill/

71. Jolly Mill
Pierce City, MO
36.89596, -94.07028
mills | limited
Jolly Mill survives from the vanished community of Jollification and has served at different times as a mill and distillery. The restored mill stands inside a public park with seasonal grinding demonstrations and an unusual local history tied to Civil War-era violence and Ozark folklore.
Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/jolly-mill

72. Dillard Mill State Historic Site
Davisville, MO
37.71774, -91.20683
mills | limited
The red Dillard Mill preserves early twentieth-century milling machinery beside Huzzah Creek in a remarkably intact rural setting. Guided access to the building is scheduled, while the surrounding historic-site grounds provide a scenic public stop.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/dillard-mill-state-historic-site

73. Bollinger Mill & Burfordville Covered Bridge
Burfordville, MO
37.36830, -89.80272
mills | limited
A four-story brick gristmill stands directly beside Missouri's oldest surviving covered bridge, creating one of the state's best preserved transportation-and-industry pairings. The bridge is pedestrian-only and the mill interior is available through scheduled tours.
Source: https://mostateparks.com/park/bollinger-mill-state-historic-site

74. Locust Creek Covered Bridge
