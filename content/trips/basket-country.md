Ohio has more of this per square mile than anywhere outside Wisconsin, and the
eastern half of the state holds an unusually good run of it.

## Start in Columbus

Fear Columbus and Haunted Hootchie are both serious seasonal haunt attractions
rather than roadside curiosities — October only, ticketed, and not somewhere to
turn up in April expecting to look around.

If you are here outside the season, start at Frazeysburg instead.

## The basket rivalry

Frazeysburg has the world's largest **apple basket**. Dresden, eight miles away,
has the world's largest **basket**.

This is not an accident. Dresden was the home of the Longaberger company, whose
headquarters was famously built as a seven-storey replica of a market basket,
and the area's whole identity was basket-making. Two towns that close with two
basket records is a rivalry, however politely nobody says so.

## Sugarcreek and Kent

The **cuckoo clock** at Sugarcreek anchors a village that leans hard into its
Swiss and Amish heritage. Then north to Kent for the **Iron Hoop**, a
five-piece sculpture by Brinsley Tyrrell, who taught at the university for
decades — a reminder that not everything large beside a road is folk art.

## Finish in Warren

The world's largest drumsticks, which is the kind of object this route
specialises in: a thing a town made because it could.

## Practical

Everything except the two haunts is free, outdoors and open year-round. The
haunts are October-only.

Two days is comfortable. One is possible if you skip Columbus.
