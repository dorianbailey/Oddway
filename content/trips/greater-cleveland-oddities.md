Northeast Ohio has an unusual density of this, and a specific character: a lot
of it is industrial money spent strangely, and a lot of the rest is a city being
funny about itself.

## Downtown first

**The Buckland Museum** holds the collection of Raymond Buckland, who brought
Wicca to the United States in the 1960s. It is a serious collection of a subject
usually handled badly.

**The Free Stamp** is a Claes Oldenburg sculpture Standard Oil commissioned for
its plaza, then declined to install after the company was bought. It sat in
storage for years before the city put it in Willard Park, on its side, where it
reads as an accusation.

## Lake View Cemetery

Two stops here and both are worth it.

**The Haserot Angel** is a 1919 bronze that has weathered into what look like
black tears running down its face. **Alan Freed's grave** is marked with a
jukebox — he coined the phrase rock and roll and was destroyed by the payola
hearings.

It is a working cemetery. Be quiet.

## East and south

**Squire's Castle** was the gatehouse of an estate Feargus Squire never
finished; his wife disliked the place and they left. What remains is a stone
shell in a Metropark that you can walk through freely.

**The Girl in Blue** at Willoughby is the grave of an unidentified woman killed
by a train in 1933, buried by the town, and identified only in 1993.

**Chef Boyardee** is buried at Chardon and was a real person: Ettore Boiardi, who
ran a hotel restaurant in Cleveland and spelled his name phonetically so
Americans could pronounce it.

## Two local legends

**Hinckley** has celebrated the return of turkey vultures every March since the
1950s. **The Peninsula Python** commemorates a large snake that escaped in 1944
and was never found, which the village has been enjoying ever since.

**Helltown** is the folklore attached to the empty houses federal land purchases
left around Boston in the 1970s. The stories are nonsense and the trespassing is
real — the visitor centre is the legal anchor.

## Practical

Two days, everything within an hour of Cleveland. Most of it is free.

Finish at **Center of the World**, a hamlet named by a landowner with a sense of
humour, and about which there is nothing else to say.
