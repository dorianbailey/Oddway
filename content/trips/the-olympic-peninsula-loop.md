US-101 runs a horseshoe around the Olympic Peninsula, and this is that road with
five reasons to stop on it. Rainforest, coast, and the specific kind of
roadside object that a town builds when the industry it lives on needs
advertising.

## Neah Bay

The northwest corner of the contiguous United States, on Makah tribal land. The
big chair sits at the end of a long drive that is worth doing for the drive.

A recreation permit is required to visit Makah lands — buy one in town, and
treat the whole area as somebody's home rather than a viewpoint, because it is.

## Into the rainforest

The Sol Duc Sasquatch is a figure in the forest of Clallam County, in exactly
the country that made the Pacific Northwest the centre of all this. Nothing is
published about who made it. The forest roads out here are remote and seasonal,
so check conditions.

The Quinault big Sitka spruce is a genuine giant and takes five minutes off the
road.

## Down the coast

South Bend calls itself the oyster capital of the world and has the sculpture to
say so. Long Beach has the frying pan, built for a clam festival — which is the
honest origin of most of these: a town needed a reason for people to come, once,
and then kept the object.

## Practical

Three days is right. It is further than it looks on a map, the road is slow, and
the weather does what it likes — this is among the wettest places in the United
States, and the rainforest is not a metaphor.

Fuel in the larger towns. Signal comes and goes across the whole peninsula.
