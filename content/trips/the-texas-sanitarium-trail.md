Texas is wide enough that a route across it is a commitment rather than a
weekend, and this one runs the full width — roughly nine hundred miles from the
mountains at El Paso to the Gulf coast plain.

## West Texas

A gravity hill on Thunderbird Drive to start, then the long empty run to
**Amarillo and Cadillac Ranch** — ten Cadillacs nose-down in a field since 1974,
buried at the angle of the Great Pyramid, and repainted by visitors constantly.
Bring spray paint; that is the point of it and always has been.

## The sanitarium markers

**Odessa** and **Post** both carry Texas historical markers where sanitariums
once stood. These are signs, not buildings — the hospitals are long gone.

They are worth stopping for anyway, because they mark something specific: the
early twentieth century belief that dry West Texas air could treat tuberculosis,
which brought a great many people out here to die a long way from home. The
markers are the only thing left of that.

## Then the giants

**Bowie** has the knife. **Lampasas** has the spur. **San Antonio** has the
Justin boots — forty feet of them, outside a shopping centre, and genuinely
enormous in person. **Seguin** has the pecan, which was the world's largest until
other towns started competing.

## Finish near Austin

**J. Lorraine** is a purpose-built ghost town near Manor, which sounds like a
contradiction and mostly works. Check opening days before driving out.

## Practical

Four days, and that is not padded. West Texas distances are real and the towns
are far apart — fuel when you can rather than when you need to.

Everything here except J. Lorraine is free and outdoors.
