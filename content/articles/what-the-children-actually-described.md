At about a quarter past seven on the evening of 12 September 1952, a group of
boys were playing on the grounds of Flatwoods Elementary School in Braxton
County, West Virginia, when a bright object crossed the sky and appeared to come
down on a hillside belonging to a farmer named G. Bailey Fisher.

They ran to tell somebody. The nearest adult was Kathleen May, mother of two of
them. She came, and brought along a seventeen-year-old National Guardsman
called Gene Lemon and the family dog, Richie.

Seven people and a dog walked up a farm road in the dark to look at what they
assumed was a meteorite.

## The description

This is the part worth sitting with, because it is unusually specific and it
has barely changed in seventy years.

A pulsing red light near the top of the rise. Lemon swung his flashlight
towards it. What the beam found was something around ten feet tall with a head
shaped like a spade — a pointed hood or cowl — and below it a body they
described as a dark, pleated metal skirt. Clawed hands. A face they remembered
as red.

There was a smell, sharp and chemical. Several of them were sick that night;
some had irritated throats for days.

They ran. A local reporter, A. Lee Stewart Jr. of the *Braxton County
Democrat*, went up the hill later the same night and found flattened grass,
marks on the ground, and the lingering odour. His account went out on the wire
services inside two days, and Flatwoods became briefly famous.

## The explanation, which is good

In 2000 the investigator Joe Nickell went to Flatwoods, walked the site, and
interviewed surviving witnesses. His account is the most convincing one
available.

The light in the sky was a **meteor** — a bright one, reported the same evening
from at least three states, seen as far away as Baltimore, three hundred miles
off. That it seemed to land on a nearby hill is a known illusion; bright
meteors are almost always described as coming down just over the next ridge.

The figure was, on this reading, a **barn owl**. Perched in the fork of a tree,
wings raised in a threat display, throwing a shadow. A barn owl's face is a
pale heart shape above a body that hangs in folds — and if you are looking for
the origin of a spade-shaped head over a pleated skirt, it is right there. Barn
owls hiss and snap. They defend nests aggressively.

Nickell also found that the hillside had a nesting pair.

## Why that is not the end of it

Because it does not really change what happened.

Seven people did walk up a dark hill expecting a crashed meteorite. Something on
that hill frightened them badly enough that a grown woman and a Guardsman ran.
Kathleen May gave interviews about it for the next fifty years and never once
softened the account.

An owl at nine feet in a torch beam, after a fireball, in the middle of the
1952 UFO flap, when everyone in America had spent the summer reading about
saucers — that is not a boring answer. It is a story about how quickly the mind
assembles a shape out of a bad light and a real fright, and it is the same
mechanism that runs underneath most of this index.

The children were not lying. They were nine to seventeen years old, they were
terrified, and they described what they saw as carefully as they could.

## Going there

The Flatwoods Monster Museum is in Sutton, on Main Street, and is free. It grew
out of a single display case in the county visitor centre and it still doubles
as one, which means the person behind the desk can point you at the rest of
Braxton County as well.
