In May 1962, Centralia burned rubbish in a pit near the Odd Fellows Cemetery,
the way it had before. The pit was an abandoned strip mine. Underneath it was an
exposed coal seam, and underneath that, the labyrinth of worked-out anthracite
tunnels the town had been built to serve.

The fire went into the seam and never came out.

It is still burning. It burns at depths reaching three hundred feet, across
something like eight miles of old workings, and by most estimates there is
enough coal down there to keep it going for another two hundred and fifty years.

## How a town finds out

Slowly, and then not slowly at all.

Residents noticed smells. Steam came up through gardens. For years it was a
nuisance rather than an emergency, and the state made several attempts to trench
ahead of the fire and cut it off, each of which underestimated how far it had
already travelled.

The moment that changed the argument was ordinary. In 1979 a gas station owner
called John Coddington lowered a thermometer into his underground fuel tank to
check a reading, and found the gasoline sitting at 172 degrees.

Carbon monoxide readings followed. Then sinkholes.

## The slow legal end

Congress put up around $42 million for relocation in 1984, and most people took
it. In 1992 the state condemned every building in the borough under eminent
domain. The Postal Service withdrew the ZIP code in 2002 — a small
administrative act that is somehow the coldest thing on the list.

A handful of residents refused throughout. In 2013 they won the right to stay
until they died, after which their properties pass to the state.

The 2020 census counted five people.

## What visitors get wrong

Two things, and both matter if you are thinking of driving there.

**The Graffiti Highway does not exist.** The abandoned mile of Route 61, buckled
by the heat and painted end to end over three decades, was buried under fill by
the landowner in April 2020 after crowds during the pandemic made the trespass
problem untenable. People still arrive looking for it. There are earthen mounds
and no-trespassing signs.

**People live there.** Centralia is a borough with residents, not a set. It gets
written up as an abandoned ghost town and photographed as one, and the handful
of people who fought for decades to stay in their own houses have to live
alongside that.

The hazards are also not decorative. Ground temperatures reach several hundred
degrees in places, subsidence can open without warning, and carbon monoxide
vents from the ground in the southern part of the borough — colourless,
odourless, and lethal in enough concentration. The state issues warnings about
this because it is real.

## The Silent Hill thing

The 2006 film borrowed Centralia's situation for its setting. The games did not
— they were made in Japan years earlier and have their own sources.

This gets repeated so often that it is worth separating, because it is the
reason a good number of people turn up. They are visiting a film location that
was never a film location, in a town where five people are trying to live.

## Going there

You can legally drive the current alignment of Route 61 through the borough and
look from the road. That is the whole of the legal access. The cemeteries are
still maintained and still used, and are not a photo opportunity.

It is a twenty-minute detour off I-81 and worth taking, provided you understand
that the interesting thing here is not a ruin. It is a place where the ordinary
explanation — a rubbish fire, a coal seam, sixty years — is worse than any story
anyone could invent about it.
