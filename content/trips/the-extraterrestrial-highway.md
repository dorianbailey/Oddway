This is the emptiest route in the index, and the one that most rewards taking
seriously. Long distances between fuel, effectively no phone signal, and two
stops where the interesting thing is a boundary you are not allowed to cross.

## The shape of it

Start at Hiko and walk the foundations of Logan City, a silver camp that lasted
four years in the 1860s. Then south on SR-375 — signed as the Extraterrestrial
Highway since 1996, which was a tourism board decision rather than anything
stranger.

The Black Mailbox is next, and you should expect not to find a mailbox. The
original belonged to a rancher named Steve Medlin, was removed years ago, and
the replacements keep getting destroyed. The one standing in mid-2025 was gone
by November. The spot is the attraction.

The north gate follows: a public dirt road meeting a fence, warning signs, and
cameras on the hill. **Stop at the signs.** Beyond them is restricted military
property, patrolled by armed contractors, and photography is prohibited. This is
not a place to test anything.

Then west to the Alien Center at Amargosa Valley for fuel and food, which after
a day on SR-375 you will need.

## Ending in the ghost towns

Rhyolite went up in the Bullfrog gold rush and emptied almost as fast. The BLM
manages it and you can walk freely among the ruins, including the bank and the
bottle house.

Leadfield is the better story. The boom lasted under a year in 1926, driven by
promotion rather than ore, and it collapsed when the ground turned out to be
worthless. It sits on the one-way Titus Canyon Road — **which is scheduled to
close from October 2026 to September 2027 for repairs**, so check before
building a day around it.

## Take this seriously

Fuel at every opportunity. Carry more water than you think. Tell somebody the
route.

The distances here are not the distances you are used to, and a breakdown on
SR-375 is a genuinely serious situation rather than an inconvenience.
