OddWay — Oklahoma Complete
120 Oklahoma stops
Stops 1–120
Prepared September 10, 2026

Access key: open = normally accessible during posted/public hours; limited = seasonal, scheduled, ticketed, cemetery-hours, appointment-only, military/security-dependent, weather/road-dependent, private-access-dependent, or check-ahead access; roadside = view from a safe lawful public area; private = private property, do not enter without permission; seasonal = primarily accessible during a defined season; view-only = observe from a lawful public viewpoint and do not enter restricted infrastructure or hazardous abandoned structures.

Research note: This set prioritizes places with a real, routable location and an OddWay-specific reason to stop. Paranormal, cryptid and supernatural claims are described as lore or reported experiences rather than established fact. Active military, archaeological, sacred, cemetery, railroad, environmental-hazard and private sites are labeled conservatively. First-party, government, museum, preservation and local-tourism sources are used wherever practical; competitor/discovery sources are retained only where a stronger primary source was not readily available. Coordinates are rounded to five decimal places and are intended as routable stop pins, not permission to cross private land or enter restricted areas.

1. Blue Whale of Catoosa
Catoosa, OK
36.19352, -95.73310
roadside-oddity | open
Built by Hugh Davis in the early 1970s, this huge smiling concrete whale rises from a pond beside historic Route 66. The former swimming-hole attraction has become one of Oklahoma's defining pieces of roadside Americana.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/listings/view.profile/id.867

35.32756, -96.92406
paranormal-lore | limited
The restored downtown theater is associated with local stories of unusual sounds and apparitions. It also survives as a tangible piece of early movie-house architecture, making it useful even for travelers who are skeptical of the ghost lore.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/articles/top-haunted-places-in-oklahoma

76. The Skirvin Hilton
Oklahoma City, OK
35.46894, -97.51389
paranormal-lore | limited
Oklahoma City's historic Skirvin hotel is tied to one of the state's best-known hotel ghost legends, usually centered on a woman called Effie. The hotel is an active business, and the story should be presented as folklore rather than verified paranormal activity.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/articles/top-haunted-places-in-oklahoma

77. Beaver Dunes Park
Beaver, OK
36.84018, -100.51382
paranormal-lore | open
The sand dunes north of Beaver are sometimes promoted in regional lore as the site of unexplained disappearances and strange phenomena, including fanciful Bermuda Triangle comparisons. The real visitable landscape is a public dune park; the supernatural layer remains folklore.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/listings/view.profile/id.406

78. Ingalls Ghost Town
Ingalls, OK
36.10180, -96.88135
ghost-town | roadside
Once a hideout area for the Doolin-Dalton gang, Ingalls was the scene of a deadly 1893 shootout between lawmen and outlaws. Only fragments of the settlement remain, making it a true place-based outlaw and ghost-town stop rather than a recreated western village.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/articles/oklahomaghosttowns

79. Adamson Ghost Town
Adamson, OK
34.92410, -95.54710
ghost-town | roadside
Adamson grew around coal mining before accidents, mine closures and declining production emptied the community. Remnants of the settlement remain in the wooded hills of southeastern Oklahoma; stay on public roads and do not enter unstable mine workings or private structures.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/articles/oklahomaghosttowns

80. Boggy Depot State Park
Atoka, OK
34.31960, -96.30718
lost-community | open
Boggy Depot was once an important Choctaw Nation town, stage stop and Civil War supply point before railroads bypassed it. The modern park preserves a cemetery, archaeological traces and interpretation from a community that largely disappeared.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/listings/view.profile/id.820

81. Doaksville Archaeological Site
Fort Towson, OK
34.03487, -95.26987
archaeology | limited
Doaksville was a major nineteenth-century Choctaw Nation town and the location where Confederate Brigadier General Stand Watie surrendered in 1865. Archaeological remains lie near Fort Towson and should be visited without disturbing the protected site.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/about/markers.php?action=Search&county=Choctaw

82. Picher Ghost Town
Picher, OK
36.98438, -94.83108
industrial-ruins | view-only
The former lead-and-zinc boomtown was largely abandoned after severe mining contamination, subsidence and a devastating 2008 tornado. It is historically compelling but environmentally hazardous; view from lawful public roads and never climb chat piles or enter abandoned structures.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/articles/oklahomaghosttowns

83. Robbers Cave State Park
Wilburton, OK
34.99501, -95.32688
outlaw-lore | open
Caves and rugged sandstone terrain in the Sans Bois Mountains are traditionally associated with outlaws including Jesse James and Belle Starr. The park gives that legend a safe, public landscape rather than directing travelers toward vague private hideout claims.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/state-parks/robbers-cave-state-park

84. Heavener Runestone Park
Heavener, OK
34.90010, -94.57800
archaeology-lore | open
A large rock bears carved characters once promoted as evidence of pre-Columbian Viking exploration in Oklahoma. Mainstream archaeology does not accept that claim, but the inscription and the history of competing interpretations make the park a strong pseudoarchaeology-and-folklore stop.
Source: https://www.heavenerrunestonepark.com/

85. Alabaster Caverns State Park
Freedom, OK
36.69824, -99.13333
geology | limited
One of the world's largest natural gypsum caves open to public touring runs beneath the plains of northwestern Oklahoma. Guided cave access and the unusual alabaster formations make it one of the state's strongest underground OddWay sites.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/state-parks/alabaster-caverns-state-park

86. Salt Plains State Park — Selenite Crystal Digging Area
Jet, OK
36.74314, -98.13228
geology | seasonal
The salt flats are famous for hourglass-shaped selenite crystals that form with trapped sand and can be dug during the legal collecting season. The stark white landscape looks more like a dried inland sea than typical Oklahoma prairie.
Source: https://www.fws.gov/refuge/salt-plains/visit-us/activities/crystal-digging

87. Gloss Mountain State Park
Fairview, OK
36.36301, -98.57908
geology | open
Selenite crystals in the mesa cap rock glint in the sun, producing the Glass versus Gloss Mountains naming story that has followed the range for generations. A steep stair-and-trail climb reaches the top of Cathedral Mountain.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/state-parks/gloss-mountain-state-park

88. Little Sahara State Park
Waynoka, OK
36.53352, -98.88139
natural-oddity | open
More than a thousand acres of shifting sand dunes create an unexpectedly desert-like landscape in northwestern Oklahoma. The park is heavily used by off-road vehicles, so pedestrians should follow current safety and access rules.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/state-parks/little-sahara-state-park

89. Black Mesa State Park
Kenton, OK
36.84059, -102.88152
natural-oddity | open
Dark volcanic rock, high-plains grassland and exceptionally dark skies make this remote Panhandle park feel unlike most of Oklahoma. It is also the practical base area for exploring the state's far western mesa country.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/state-parks/black-mesa-state-park

90. Black Mesa Summit Trailhead
Kenton, OK
36.96055, -102.96362
geographic-oddity | limited
An 8.4-mile round-trip trail climbs from this public trailhead to Oklahoma's highest point near the New Mexico border. The summit itself is remote, so the trailhead is the safer routable pin; carry water and check weather before attempting the hike.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/listings/view.profile/id.672

91. Holy City of the Wichitas
Lawton, OK
34.74257, -98.59034
religious-oddity | limited
Stone buildings and walls create a miniature biblical landscape in the Wichita Mountains, built as the setting for a long-running Easter passion play. Even outside performances, the fortress-like complex is one of Oklahoma's strangest constructed environments.
Source: https://theholycitylawton.com/

92. Parallel Forest
Lawton, OK
34.75070, -98.58034
natural-oddity | open
Thousands of red cedars were planted in eerily straight rows about six feet apart as part of erosion-control work. The perfect spacing gives an ordinary forest an unnatural visual rhythm that has also inspired local ghost stories.
Source: https://www.fws.gov/refuge/wichita-mountains

93. Collings Castle
Davis, OK
34.42707, -97.14729
architectural-ruins | limited
A hand-built stone castle from the Great Depression era clings to a steep hillside inside Turner Falls Park. Narrow openings, rough masonry and partial deterioration make it look far older than its twentieth-century construction date.
Source: https://www.turnerfallspark.com/

94. Cavanal Hill — World's Highest Hill Claim
Poteau, OK
35.07093, -94.67939
geographic-oddity | open
Cavanal Hill is locally promoted as the world's highest hill because its rise is often cited at just under the traditional 2,000-foot threshold for a mountain. The classification is promotional rather than a universal geographic rule, but the claim has become part of the place's identity.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/listings/view.profile/id.1209

95. Redbud Park Outlaw Cave
Marlow, OK
34.64778, -97.95099
outlaw-lore | open
A small cave in Redbud Park is tied by local tradition to Marlow-area outlaw stories and the Marlow brothers. The public park gives travelers a concrete, accessible anchor for legends that otherwise blur into regional folklore.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/listings/view.profile/id.22827

96. Museum of Osteology
Oklahoma City, OK
35.36483, -97.44184
odd-museum | open
Hundreds of real animal skeletons fill a museum devoted almost entirely to comparative anatomy. The dense displays of skulls, articulated skeletons and unusual species make it one of Oklahoma's clearest cabinet-of-curiosities style museums.
Source: https://www.skeletonmuseum.com/

97. Mabee-Gerrer Museum of Art — Egyptian Collection
Shawnee, OK
35.36607, -96.95508
odd-museum | open
This university museum contains an unexpectedly strong collection of Egyptian antiquities, including a mummy, alongside art and archaeological material from around the world. The Egyptian galleries give it a much stranger profile than a typical regional art museum.
Source: https://www.mgmoa.org/

98. American Pigeon Museum & Library
Oklahoma City, OK
35.53632, -97.47135
odd-museum | limited
A museum devoted to pigeons documents racing, military messenger birds, fancy breeds and the long relationship between humans and domesticated pigeons. It is one of the state's most narrowly focused specialty museums.
Source: https://www.theamericanpigeonmuseum.org/

99. American Banjo Museum
Oklahoma City, OK
35.46677, -97.51154
odd-museum | open
Hundreds of ornate banjos trace the instrument from minstrel-era history through jazz, bluegrass and modern performance. Elaborately decorated instruments and a highly specific collecting focus make the museum an unusually strong niche stop.
Source: https://americanbanjomuseum.com/

100. Oklahoma Frontier Drugstore Museum
Guthrie, OK
35.87819, -97.42724
medical-history | limited
Historic pharmacy fixtures, medicine bottles, patent remedies and equipment fill a recreated frontier drugstore environment. The displays preserve the sometimes-unsettling overlap between medicine, advertising and commerce in early Oklahoma.
Source: https://www.okpharmheritage.com/

101. Oklahoma Museum of Telephone History
Oklahoma City, OK
35.47104, -97.51523
odd-museum | limited
Switchboards, telephones, signs and telecommunications equipment document the rapidly changing technology behind everyday conversation. The specialized collection turns obsolete infrastructure into a surprisingly visual museum.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/Roadside_Oddities

102. Twister Museum
Wakita, OK
36.88041, -97.92468
film-oddity | limited
Wakita served as a major filming location for the 1996 movie Twister, and this small museum preserves props, photographs and production memorabilia. The town itself still carries visual traces of its role in the tornado film.
Source: https://twistercountry.com/

103. Toy & Action Figure Museum
Pauls Valley, OK
34.74012, -97.21957
odd-museum | open
Thousands of action figures, toys and comic-related objects fill a museum built around the visual history of modern character merchandising. Displays range from superhero collections to an enormous staged action-figure diorama.
Source: https://www.toyandactionfiguremuseum.com/

104. J.M. Davis Arms & Historical Museum
Claremore, OK
36.31460, -95.61503
odd-museum | open
The museum holds one of the world's largest privately assembled firearms collections alongside swords, outlaw artifacts, musical instruments and assorted curiosities. Its sheer density and eccentric breadth make it more cabinet collection than conventional weapons museum.
Source: https://www.thegunmuseum.com/

105. Tom Mix Museum
Dewey, OK
36.79832, -95.93823
pop-culture | limited
This museum is devoted to silent-film cowboy star Tom Mix, who worked in the Dewey area before becoming an early Hollywood western icon. Costumes, saddles, photographs and memorabilia preserve the strange bridge between real ranch culture and manufactured screen mythology.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/sites/tommix

106. Stafford Air & Space Museum
Weatherford, OK
35.53889, -98.66778
space-history | open
Named for astronaut Thomas P. Stafford, this museum holds aircraft, rocket engines, spacecraft-related hardware and thousands of aviation artifacts. Full-scale machines and spaceflight objects make it substantially more tactile than a general science museum.
Source: https://www.staffordmuseum.org/

107. Seaba Station Motorcycle Museum
Warwick, OK
35.68652, -97.00026
transportation-oddity | limited
A restored 1920s Route 66 service station now contains dozens of motorcycles ranging from early machines to unusual custom bikes. The reuse of the roadside station makes the building and collection equally important to the stop.
Source: https://seabastation.com/

108. Oklahoma Route 66 Museum
Clinton, OK
35.50657, -98.98690
route-66-history | open
Immersive galleries move through different eras of Route 66 using vehicles, signs, music and recreated roadside environments. The museum is one of the strongest places in the state for understanding the highway beyond individual roadside photo stops.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/sites/visit-route-66

109. National Route 66 & Transportation Museum
Elk City, OK
35.41220, -99.43585
route-66-history | open
This large museum complex interprets all eight Route 66 states through recreated scenes, vehicles and transportation exhibits. A giant roadside figure at the entrance and an old-town complex extend the experience outside the main galleries.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/listings/view.profile/id.5284

110. Chandler Route 66 Interpretive Center
Chandler, OK
35.70959, -96.87702
route-66-history | open
Housed in a former National Guard armory, the center uses video installations, unusual seating and oral-history material to interpret Route 66. Its presentation is more experiential than a conventional display-case museum.
Source: https://www.route66interpretivecenter.org/

111. No Man's Land Museum
Goodwell, OK
36.59471, -101.63520
odd-museum | limited
The museum focuses on the Oklahoma Panhandle, once widely called No Man's Land, with archaeology, fossils, agricultural material and regional artifacts. Its location near the center of the Panhandle gives context to one of the state's strangest geographic histories.
Source: https://www.opsu.edu/no-mans-land-museum/

112. Eskridge Hotel Museum
Wynnewood, OK
34.64294, -97.16967
weird-history | limited
This restored historic hotel preserves period rooms, local artifacts and the atmosphere of a small-town lodging house from another era. The building's layered commercial and community history makes it more immersive than a standard local-history room.
Source: https://www.travelok.com/listings/search/3?city=Wynnewood

113. Spiro Mounds Archaeological Center
Spiro, OK
35.31173, -94.56843
archaeology | limited
One of the most important Mississippian ceremonial centers west of the Mississippi River survives here as protected earthworks and archaeological landscape. The museum and trails interpret a complex Indigenous community; mounds and cultural resources must not be climbed, collected from or disturbed.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/sites/visit-spiro-mounds

114. Washita Battlefield National Historic Site
Cheyenne, OK
35.61672, -99.68630
dark-history | open
The National Park Service interprets the 1868 attack by Lt. Col. George A. Custer's forces on a Southern Cheyenne village led by Peace Chief Black Kettle. Begin at the visitor center and follow designated trails to approach the landscape with appropriate historical and cultural context.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/waba/

115. Honey Springs Battlefield
Checotah, OK
35.52356, -95.48512
military-history | limited
The 1863 Battle of Honey Springs was the largest Civil War engagement in Indian Territory and involved Native, Black and white soldiers. A modern visitor center and preserved battlefield landscape interpret a conflict often omitted from standard Civil War tourism.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/sites/visit-honey-springs

116. Fort Gibson Historic Site
Fort Gibson, OK
35.80399, -95.25699
military-history | limited
Established in 1824, Fort Gibson became a major U.S. military post on the western frontier and later figured in Indian Removal and Civil War history. Reconstructed and original-era structures make the complex a tangible counterpart to nearby archaeological and tribal-history sites.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/sites/fortgibson

117. Fort Towson Historic Site
Fort Towson, OK
34.02788, -95.25634
military-history | limited
Founded in 1824 near the Red River borderlands, Fort Towson served the U.S. Army, the Choctaw Nation region and later Confederate forces. The site also connects directly with nearby Doaksville, where Stand Watie surrendered in 1865.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/sites/forttowson

118. Sod House Museum
Aline, OK
36.47216, -98.42572
architectural-oddity | limited
A genuine 1894 sod house remains preserved beneath a protective structure on its original farmstead. Visitors can step inside a surviving example of the earth-built homes used by settlers where lumber was scarce.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/sites/visit-sod-house

119. Pioneer Woman Museum & Statue
Ponca City, OK
36.71049, -97.06558
monumental-art | limited
A large bronze Pioneer Woman leads a child beside a museum devoted to the experiences and mythology of women in Oklahoma settlement history. The monumental statue and highly focused museum make the complex more distinctive than a general pioneer museum.
Source: https://www.okhistory.org/sites/pioneerwoman

120. Standing Bear Park, Museum & Monument
Ponca City, OK
36.68285, -97.07595
indigenous-history | open
This public park and museum honor Ponca Chief Standing Bear, whose 1879 federal court case affirmed that Native Americans were persons entitled to seek habeas corpus protection. A large statue and interpretive spaces make the site both a legal-history landmark and a major Indigenous cultural stop.
Source: https://www.standingbearpark.com/
