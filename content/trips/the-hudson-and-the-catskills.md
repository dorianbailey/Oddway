The Hudson Valley has been generating this material for longer than the country
has existed, and unusually, a lot of it is still standing.

## Start where the story does

**Sleepy Hollow.** Washington Irving published the Headless Horseman in 1820,
and the valley has been trading on it ever since — but the marker is worth
stopping at because the story is genuinely from here rather than attached to it
afterwards, which is not true of most American legends.

**The Leatherman** at Ossining is the better one. A real man who walked a
365-mile circuit through New York and Connecticut in the 1880s in a handmade
suit of heavy leather, on a schedule regular enough that households set out food
for him. Nobody ever established who he was.

## Up the river

**The Armour-Stiner Octagon House** at Irvington is the only known
eight-sided domed Victorian house in the world, which is a specific enough claim
to be true.

**Bannerman Castle** on Pollepel Island is a munitions dealer's fantasy
warehouse, built from 1901 and never finished. It burned in 1969 and partly
collapsed in 2009. Boat tours only, seasonal — book ahead.

**Pine Bush** spent the 1980s and 90s as one of the most reported UFO areas in
the eastern United States and now has the museum.

## The Catskills

**The Kaatskill Kaleidoscope** at Mount Tremper is built inside a converted
grain silo and is the largest in the world.

**Overlook Mountain House** is a hotel that burned three times; the concrete
1920s attempt was abandoned unfinished and its shell is a two-mile uphill walk
from the road.

**Opus 40** finishes it: six and a half acres of bluestone that the sculptor
Harvey Fite spent thirty-seven years fitting together without mortar. He
intended it as a setting for his statues and eventually realised it was the
work.

## Practical

Three days. Bannerman and Overlook both need planning — one is a boat, the other
is a climb.

Autumn is the obvious season and everyone knows it. The valley is quieter and
just as good in May.
