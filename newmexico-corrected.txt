OddWay — New Mexico Complete
160 New Mexico stops
Stops 1–160
Prepared September 10, 2026

Access key: open = normally accessible during posted/public hours; limited = seasonal, scheduled, ticketed, cemetery-hours, appointment-only, culturally sensitive, military/security-dependent, weather/road-dependent, private-access-dependent, or check-ahead access; roadside = view from a safe lawful public area; private = private property, do not enter without permission; seasonal = primarily accessible during a defined season; view-only = observe from a lawful public viewpoint and do not enter restricted structures, property, infrastructure, or culturally closed areas.

Research note: This set prioritizes places with a real, routable location and an OddWay-specific reason to stop. Paranormal and UFO claims are described as lore, reports, or alleged events rather than fact. Active military, archaeological, sacred, cemetery, railroad, tribal, national-park, and private sites are labeled conservatively. First-party, government, museum, preservation, or local-tourism sources are used wherever practical; competitor/discovery sources are retained only when a stronger primary source was not readily available. Coordinates are rounded to five decimal places and are intended as routable stop pins, not permission to cross private land or enter restricted areas.

Current-access exclusions/checks made for this September 2026 list: Sunspot Solar Observatory is excluded because the observatory, visitor center, grounds and nearby trails are closed indefinitely after the January 2026 mercury leak. Roswell Museum, Goddard Planetarium and the Anderson Museum of Contemporary Art are excluded from normal routing because of flood-recovery closures. El Camino Real Historic Trail Site is excluded because its current visitor page says it is temporarily closed until further notice. White Sands' Interdune Boardwalk is excluded because of its current closure. Lincoln's San Juan Church is not used as an active stop because of the posted structural/safety closure. Kasha-Katuwe Tent Rocks is retained as limited/reservation-dependent, Trinity Site as an extremely limited open-house stop, and White Sands Missile Range Museum as security-dependent.

1. International UFO Museum & Research Center
Roswell, NM
33.39430, -104.52220
ufo-history | open
Roswell's best-known UFO museum centers on the 1947 Roswell Incident and the decades of documents, witness accounts, photographs and popular culture that followed it. The museum treats the city's defining UFO story as the main attraction rather than a side exhibit.
Source: https://www.roswellufomuseum.com/

2. Roswell UFO Spacewalk
Roswell, NM
33.39416, -104.52183
outsider-art | open
Artist Larry Welz built this black-light walk-through from recycled, found and donated materials, turning a downtown storefront into a glowing alien landscape. It is part UFO attraction, part handmade art environment.
Source: https://www.roswellufospacewalk.com/

3. Alien Zone Area 51
Roswell, NM
33.39505, -104.52218
ufo-roadside | open
A downtown alien-themed photo attraction fills staged rooms with saucers, extraterrestrials and deliberately campy Roswell scenery. It is a separate stop from the UFO museum and one of the city's strongest pieces of roadside alien kitsch.
Source: https://www.roswellufomuseum.com/area-attractions

4. Miniatures & Curious Collections Museum
Roswell, NM
33.39678, -104.52334
odd-museums | limited
This small museum preserves elaborate miniatures alongside rotating displays of objects considered unusual, antique or simply interesting. Its collection grew from Roswell's Los Pocos Locos Miniature Society and is intentionally broader than a conventional dollhouse museum.
Source: https://www.miniaturesandcuriouscollectionsmuseum.org/

5. Walker Aviation Museum
Roswell, NM
33.30195, -104.52944
aviation-history | limited
Inside the Roswell Air Center terminal, this museum preserves artifacts and photographs from Roswell Army Air Field and Walker Air Force Base. The setting is especially fitting because the modern airport occupies the former military base.
Source: https://www.wafbmuseum.org/

6. McBride Museum at New Mexico Military Institute
Roswell, NM
33.40902, -104.52268
military-history | limited
The New Mexico Military Institute museum preserves uniforms, weapons, photographs and institutional history from one of the country's unusual state-supported military schools. Visitors should check campus access and museum hours before arriving.
Source: https://www.nmmi.edu/mcbride-museum/

7. Roswell Space Toy Museum
Roswell, NM
33.39654, -104.52217
odd-museums | open
Vintage rockets, robots and space toys from the 1930s through the 1960s fill this downtown museum. The collection shows how science fiction and the Space Age were packaged for children long before Roswell's modern alien tourism boom.
Source: https://www.roswell-nm.gov/1817/Museum-Day-Roswell-2026

8. UFO McDonald's
Roswell, NM
33.40188, -104.52242
roadside-oddity | open
Roswell's downtown McDonald's is shaped and decorated to resemble a flying saucer, complete with space-themed exterior details. It is a functioning restaurant, but the building itself has become part of the city's alien-roadside landscape.
Source: https://www.newmexico.org/places-to-visit/southeast/roswell/

9. Alleged UFO Skip Site
Corona, NM
33.95219, -105.33121
ufo-history | limited
BLM identifies a lawful public-land hiking access point connected with an alleged 1947 UFO 'skip' or crash story near Corona. Vehicle approaches used historically cross private property, so OddWay should route only to the BLM access and never across ranch land.
Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/alleged-ufo-skip-site

10. Bottomless Lakes State Park
Roswell, NM
33.32450, -104.33200
geology | open
A chain of deep sinkholes filled with green-blue water gave rise to the 'bottomless' name east of Roswell. The lakes are actually measurable, but their steep-sided geology and unusual desert setting made the legend stick.
Source: https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/spd/find-a-park/bottomless-lakes-state-park/

11. World's Largest Pistachio
Alamogordo, NM
32.81282, -105.99143
roadside-oddity | open
A giant concrete pistachio rises outside PistachioLand as an intentionally oversized monument to the local nut crop. The sculpture is one of southern New Mexico's most recognizable classic roadside photo stops.
Source: https://pistachioland.com/

12. New Mexico Museum of Space History
Alamogordo, NM
32.92111, -105.92111
space-history | open
This hillside museum traces rockets, satellites, early spaceflight and New Mexico's unusually deep connection to missile testing and space exploration. Its International Space Hall of Fame makes it more than a local aerospace collection.
Source: https://nmspacemuseum.org/

13. Toy Train Depot
Alamogordo, NM
32.91412, -105.96101
odd-museums | limited
A former railroad depot houses model trains, railroad artifacts and miniature layouts, preserving Alamogordo's rail history at a much smaller scale. Operating hours are limited, so it is best treated as a check-ahead museum stop.
Source: https://toytraindepot.org/

14. Shroud Exhibit and Museum
Alamogordo, NM
32.92695, -105.95730
religious-mysteries | limited
This unusual museum examines the Shroud of Turin through a life-size research photograph, historical material and a VP-8 image-analysis display. The original shroud is not here; the attraction focuses on the scientific and religious mystery surrounding it.
Source: https://shroudnm.com/

15. White Sands National Park Visitor Center & Dunes
Alamogordo, NM
32.77908, -106.17119
geologic-oddities | open
The world's largest gypsum dunefield creates a landscape that resembles snow despite lying in the Chihuahuan Desert. Dunes Drive begins at the historic visitor center and leads directly into miles of brilliant white gypsum.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/whsa/

16. Alkali Flat Trail
Alamogordo, NM
32.79840, -106.23370
geologic-oddities | limited
This strenuous loop crosses the heart of White Sands where the gypsum dunes are largest and the developed landscape disappears. The route is exposed, waterless and occasionally affected by missile-range closures, so conditions must be checked before hiking.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/whsa/planyourvisit/alkali-flat-trail.htm

17. White Sands Missile Range Museum
White Sands Missile Range, NM
32.38473, -106.47841
military-history | limited
Missiles, rockets and testing hardware document the history of the enormous White Sands range, including weapons development and early American space work. The museum is inside an active military installation, so current security and entry rules apply.
Source: https://history.army.mil/Army-Museum-Enterprise/Find-an-Army-Museum/White-Sands-Missile-Range-Museum/

18. Trinity Site Ground Zero
White Sands Missile Range, NM
33.67728, -106.47538
nuclear-history | limited
A lava-rock obelisk marks ground zero of the July 16, 1945 Trinity test, the world's first detonation of a nuclear weapon. Public access is extremely limited; in 2026 the announced open house is October 17.
Source: https://home.army.mil/wsmr/index.php/contact/public-affairs-office/trinity-site-open-house

19. Old Apple Barn & Giant Apple Boy
High Rolls, NM
32.95232, -105.82415
roadside-oddity | open
A 12-foot handmade Apple Boy waves above this mountain roadside store, a deliberately modern take on the fiberglass giants of older American highways. The barn itself is packed with local foods, antiques and souvenir oddities.
Source: https://www.oldapplebarn.com/

20. Mexican Canyon Trestle
Cloudcroft, NM
32.96373, -105.74768
railroad-history | roadside
This preserved 1899 wooden railroad trestle once carried trains up the steep Sacramento Mountains toward Cloudcroft. A lawful highway overlook gives travelers a clear view without entering the historic structure.
Source: https://www.fs.usda.gov/recarea/lincoln/recarea/?recid=34212

21. Three Rivers Petroglyph Site
Three Rivers, NM
33.34423, -106.00738
rock-art | limited
More than 21,000 Jornada Mogollon petroglyphs cover the basalt ridge here, including animals, human figures and complex geometric designs. Visitors must stay on established access routes and never touch or damage the rock art.
Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/three-rivers-petroglyph-site

22. Valley of Fires Recreation Area
Carrizozo, NM
33.68480, -105.92001
volcanic-geology | open
A black lava flow cuts across the Tularosa Basin like a frozen river of rock. The Malpais Nature Trail lets visitors walk directly over a flow created roughly 5,000 years ago, an unusually young landscape by geologic standards.
Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/valley-fires-recreation-area

23. White Oaks Historic Ghost Town
White Oaks, NM
33.74646, -105.73420
ghost-towns | open
White Oaks boomed after gold discoveries in the late nineteenth century, then faded when the railroad bypassed it. Historic buildings and a tiny surviving community make it a living ghost-town landscape rather than an abandoned ruin field.
Source: https://www.newmexico.org/places-to-visit/ghost-towns/white-oaks/

24. Smokey Bear Historical Park
Capitan, NM
33.54536, -105.57265
weird-history | open
The real black bear cub who became the living symbol of Smokey Bear is buried here in his hometown region. Exhibits connect the 1950 Capitan Gap fire, the rescued cub and the national wildfire-prevention icon that grew from his story.
Source: https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/sfd/smokey-bear-historical-park/

25. Fort Stanton Historic Site
Fort Stanton, NM
33.49574, -105.52205
dark-history | limited
Fort Stanton's unusually intact parade ground has served as an Army post, tuberculosis hospital, CCC camp and World War II internment site for German sailors. Those overlapping uses give the complex one of the stranger institutional histories in New Mexico.
Source: https://www.dca.nm.gov/historic-sites/fort-stanton

26. Lincoln County Courthouse Museum
Lincoln, NM
33.49274, -105.38366
outlaw-history | limited
Billy the Kid escaped from this former courthouse in 1881 after killing two deputies, making the building one of the most concrete surviving sites in the Lincoln County War story. It remains part of the state-run Lincoln Historic Site.
Source: https://www.dca.nm.gov/historic-sites/lincoln

27. Tunstall Store
Lincoln, NM
33.49250, -105.38305
outlaw-history | limited
Merchant John Tunstall's store survives from the violent Lincoln County War era and still contains period material tied to the conflict. Tunstall's killing helped ignite the feud that made Billy the Kid nationally famous.
Source: https://www.dca.nm.gov/historic-sites/lincoln

28. The Torreon at Lincoln
Lincoln, NM
33.49218, -105.38236
frontier-history | limited
This round stone defensive tower is one of Lincoln's oldest surviving structures and was used by residents as a refuge during raids. Its compact fortified form is a stark contrast to the adobe commercial buildings along the main street.
Source: https://www.dca.nm.gov/historic-sites/lincoln

29. Billy the Kid Grave
Fort Sumner, NM
34.40349, -104.19483
outlaw-history | limited
Billy the Kid is buried in the old Fort Sumner cemetery beneath a heavily protected grave marker. The site has become a pilgrimage point for outlaw history, but it remains an active historic cemetery and should be visited respectfully.
Source: https://www.newmexico.org/places-to-visit/regions/southeast/fort-sumner/

30. Billy the Kid Museum
Fort Sumner, NM
34.47136, -104.24576
odd-museums | open
This long-running private museum holds firearms, photographs, saddles and frontier artifacts connected with Billy the Kid and the surrounding region. Its dense, old-school collection feels closer to a roadside cabinet of curiosities than a polished state museum.
Source: https://www.billythekidmuseumfortsumner.com/

31. Bosque Redondo Memorial at Fort Sumner Historic Site
Fort Sumner, NM
34.40233, -104.19618
dark-history | limited
The memorial interprets the forced removal and imprisonment of Navajo and Mescalero Apache people at Bosque Redondo in the 1860s. It is an essential, sobering counterpart to the outlaw tourism found elsewhere around Fort Sumner.
Source: https://www.dca.nm.gov/historic-sites/bosque-redondo-memorial-at-fort-sumner-historic-site

32. Blackwater Draw National Historic Landmark
Portales, NM
34.27830, -103.31950
archaeology | seasonal
Excavations at Blackwater Draw helped define the Clovis culture through mammoth remains, stone tools and deeply layered evidence of human occupation. The outdoor archaeological site has seasonal hours separate from the university museum in Portales.
Source: https://www.bwdarchaeology.com/

33. Blackwater Draw Museum
Portales, NM
34.17637, -103.34710
archaeology | limited
This Eastern New Mexico University museum displays material from the Blackwater Draw excavations and explains why 'Clovis' became a foundational term in North American archaeology. It is a separate indoor stop from the excavation site itself.
Source: https://www.bwdarchaeology.com/

34. Norman Petty Recording Studios
Clovis, NM
34.40452, -103.22120
music-history | limited
Norman Petty's small studio is where Buddy Holly, Roy Orbison and other early rock performers recorded in the 1950s. The original equipment and room survive, but access is generally by appointment rather than ordinary walk-in hours.
Source: https://normanpettystudios.com/

35. Norman & Vi Petty Rock 'n' Roll Museum
Clovis, NM
34.40403, -103.20495
music-history | limited
This downtown collection interprets the Clovis recording scene through instruments, photographs and material connected with Norman Petty and the musicians who worked with him. It complements the original studio without duplicating the same visitor experience.
Source: https://www.cityofclovis.org/parks-and-recreation/museums/

36. Blue Swallow Motel
Tucumcari, NM
35.17187, -103.71642
route-66 | roadside
One of Route 66's best-preserved neon motels still operates beneath its famous blue-swallow sign. Travelers can appreciate the exterior from public areas, but guest spaces remain private unless staying at the property.
Source: https://blueswallowmotel.com/

37. Tee Pee Curios
Tucumcari, NM
35.17170, -103.71480
route-66 | open
A concrete teepee entrance and vintage neon mark this surviving Route 66 curio shop. The building began as a gas station and now functions as a compact piece of roadside architecture and souvenir culture.
Source: https://tee-pee-curios.square.site/

38. New Mexico Route 66 Museum
Tucumcari, NM
35.16997, -103.74190
odd-museums | limited
Vintage cars, signs, photographs and memorabilia document New Mexico's stretch of Route 66 inside the convention-center complex. It gives useful context for the surviving motels and neon scattered across Tucumcari.
Source: https://nmroute66museum.org/

39. Tucumcari Historical Museum
Tucumcari, NM
35.16904, -103.72585
odd-museums | limited
An old school building is crowded with regional objects ranging from ranching equipment and military material to household artifacts. The eclectic room-by-room display makes it substantially stranger than a conventional single-theme history museum.
Source: https://tucumcarinm.com/museums/

40. Tucumcari Railroad Museum
Tucumcari, NM
35.17072, -103.72689
railroad-history | limited
The restored depot and railroad collection preserve the transportation system that shaped Tucumcari before and during the Route 66 era. Rail artifacts and the trackside setting make the building itself part of the exhibit.
Source: https://www.tucumcarirailroadmuseum.org/

41. Mesalands Dinosaur Museum
Tucumcari, NM
35.17345, -103.72396
paleontology | open
Full-size dinosaur skeletons, fossils and active paleontology work fill this college museum. The collection is particularly strong in fossils from the surrounding high plains, making it more than a generic dinosaur display.
Source: https://www.mesalands.edu/community/dinosaur-museum/

42. Route 66 Monument
Tucumcari, NM
35.17146, -103.74294
roadside-oddity | open
Tom Coffin's sculptural Route 66 monument uses the rear half of a classic car rising from a stylized roadway. It sits outside the convention center and functions as one of Tucumcari's easiest roadside photo stops.
Source: https://tucumcarinm.com/

43. Cuervo Ghost Town
Cuervo, NM
35.05695, -104.40770
ghost-towns | roadside
I-40 cut through this former Route 66 railroad settlement, leaving abandoned houses and commercial ruins on both sides of the highway. Most structures are private or unstable, so OddWay should route to lawful public roads only and never encourage entry.
Source: https://www.newmexico.org/places-to-visit/ghost-towns/

44. Route 66 Auto Museum
Santa Rosa, NM
34.94434, -104.70272
odd-museums | open
Dozens of restored classic cars, hot rods, signs and Route 66 memorabilia fill this roadside museum. The collection fits Santa Rosa's position as one of the better-preserved automobile-culture stops on the historic highway.
Source: https://www.route66automuseum.com/

45. Blue Hole
Santa Rosa, NM
34.94045, -104.68284
natural-oddities | open
An artesian spring forms an almost perfectly round, deep blue pool in the middle of high-desert Santa Rosa. Its constant clear water and depth make it a nationally known diving site as well as a natural roadside oddity.
Source: https://www.santarosanm.gov/blue-hole

47. Clayton Lake Dinosaur Trackway
Clayton, NM
36.57315, -103.30480
paleontology | limited
Hundreds of dinosaur footprints are exposed in sandstone beside Clayton Lake, including tracks left by several different kinds of dinosaurs. The trackway is reached by a short trail and is one of the clearest in-place fossil stops in the state.
Source: https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/spd/find-a-park/clayton-lake-state-park-and-dinosaur-trackways/

48. Capulin Volcano National Monument
Capulin, NM
36.78110, -103.97105
volcanic-geology | limited
A paved road spirals toward the rim of an extinct cinder cone rising abruptly from the plains. Weather, vehicle restrictions and seasonal conditions can affect summit access even when the monument itself is open.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/cavo/

49. Fort Union National Monument
Watrous, NM
35.90722, -105.00952
ruins | open
Massive adobe wall fragments trace the footprint of the largest nineteenth-century military fort in the Southwest. The wind-scoured ruins preserve a strangely skeletal military landscape on the open plains.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/foun/

50. St. James Hotel
Cimarron, NM
36.51056, -104.91574
paranormal | limited
This 1870s hotel leans heavily into stories of gunfighters, violent deaths and reported paranormal activity. It remains a functioning hotel, so hauntings should be presented as property lore and access limited to public or booked areas.
Source: https://www.exstjames.com/

51. Dawson Cemetery
Dawson, NM
36.65532, -104.76777
dark-history | limited
Rows of white crosses mark victims of catastrophic coal-mine explosions that devastated the company town of Dawson in 1913 and 1923. Access may be restricted and permission requirements can change, so travelers must obey gates and posted rules.
Source: https://www.newmexico.org/places-to-visit/ghost-towns/dawson/

52. Carlsbad Caverns National Park
Carlsbad, NM
32.17533, -104.44350
caves | limited
Carlsbad Caverns contains an enormous underground chamber system formed in Permian limestone, including the famous Big Room. Timed entry and cave conditions make this a reservation/check-ahead stop rather than an unrestricted roadside attraction.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/cave/

53. Living Desert Zoo & Gardens State Park
Carlsbad, NM
32.44173, -104.25336
natural-history | open
This hilltop state park concentrates Chihuahuan Desert plants and animals into a walkable collection overlooking Carlsbad. Its mountain lions, reptiles, native plants and unusual desert adaptations give it a strong regional natural-history fit.
Source: https://www.emnrd.nm.gov/spd/find-a-park/living-desert-zoo-and-gardens-state-park/

54. Sitting Bull Falls
Carlsbad, NM
32.24326, -104.69736
natural-oddities | limited
A spring-fed waterfall and clear pools appear unexpectedly in a dry canyon west of Carlsbad. Seasonal water conditions, fire restrictions and capacity limits can affect access, making it a check-ahead desert-oasis stop.
Source: https://www.fs.usda.gov/recarea/lincoln/recarea/?recid=34238

55. Recycled Roadrunner
Las Cruces, NM
32.29342, -106.81586
outsider-art | open
A giant roadrunner sculpture built largely from discarded shoes, electronics, metal and other trash stands at the I-10 rest area west of Las Cruces. Its recycled construction turns a familiar state bird into one of New Mexico's strangest public artworks.

Las Cruces, NM
32.31186, -106.80762
roadside-oddity | roadside
A roughly 47-foot chile pepper towers beside a motel in Las Cruces as an exaggerated tribute to southern New Mexico's chile culture. It can be photographed from lawful public or customer-access areas without entering private guest spaces.

57. Zuhl Museum
Las Cruces, NM
32.28222, -106.74735
odd-museums | limited
New Mexico State University displays petrified wood, fossils and polished mineral specimens from the Zuhl collection in a museum that feels halfway between geology lab and gemstone gallery. Campus hours should be checked before visiting.

58. New Mexico Farm & Ranch Heritage Museum
Las Cruces, NM
32.29843, -106.74093
odd-museums | open
This large state museum combines agricultural machinery, livestock, historic buildings and exhibits on thousands of years of farming in the Rio Grande valley. Working animals and outdoor structures make it more immersive than a standard history museum.
Source: https://nmfarmandranchmuseum.org/

59. Taylor-Mesilla Historic Site
Mesilla, NM
32.27395, -106.80062
historic-homes | limited
The restored Taylor home preserves an unusually rich collection of New Mexican, Mexican and borderlands art assembled by J. Paul and Mary Daniels Taylor. State-site hours and guided access can vary.

60. Mesilla Plaza
Mesilla, NM
32.27422, -106.80083
weird-history | open
This compact plaza has witnessed territorial politics, stagecoach traffic and the trial of Billy the Kid. Its surviving adobe streetscape makes nineteenth-century borderland history unusually tangible in the middle of a modern metro area.
Source: https://www.mesillanm.gov/

61. Basilica of San Albino
Mesilla, NM
32.27404, -106.80031
religious-history | limited
The present church anchors Mesilla Plaza on a site associated with parish life since the 1850s. Its twin towers and borderlands history make it one of the strongest architectural landmarks in the historic district; visitors should respect worship services.
Source: https://sanalbino.org/

62. Double Eagle Restaurant Ghost Lore
Mesilla, NM
32.27448, -106.80059
paranormal | limited
The historic adobe property is associated with a long-running local legend about two young lovers killed there and later reported as apparitions. It is an operating restaurant, so OddWay should present the story as lore and respect normal business access.
Source: https://www.doubleeagleonline.com/

63. La Posta de Mesilla
Mesilla, NM
32.27461, -106.80099
route-history | open
A nineteenth-century stagecoach stop evolved into a sprawling restaurant filled with historic rooms, tropical birds and borderlands memorabilia. The building's layered stagecoach and commercial history makes the property a destination beyond the meal itself.
Source: https://www.laposta-de-mesilla.com/

64. Pat Garrett Grave
Las Cruces, NM
32.30120, -106.78546
outlaw-history | limited
Sheriff Pat Garrett, best known for killing Billy the Kid, is buried in the Masonic Cemetery in Las Cruces. The grave is a legitimate outlaw-history stop but should be treated with normal cemetery etiquette and lawful visiting hours.

65. Pat Garrett Death Site Marker
Las Cruces, NM
32.36620, -106.71715
outlaw-history | roadside
A simple desert marker preserves the approximate place connected with Pat Garrett's 1908 killing east of Las Cruces. The site lies near modern development and private land, so visitors should remain at the lawful marker/access point.

66. Dripping Springs Natural Area Historic Ruins
Las Cruces, NM
32.32957, -106.57521
ruins | limited
Ruins of the Van Patten Mountain Camp and later sanatorium sit below the Organ Mountains beside a spring-fed canyon. The blend of resort remains, health-history ruins and dramatic geology makes this one of southern New Mexico's strongest OddWay hikes.
Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/dripping-springs-natural-area

67. La Cueva Rock Shelter
Las Cruces, NM
32.33495, -106.58333
archaeology | limited
This rock shelter shows evidence of human use stretching back thousands of years and later became associated with the eccentric hermit Giovanni Maria Agostini. It is reached on foot from the Dripping Springs area; artifacts must never be disturbed.
Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/la-cueva

68. Prehistoric Trackways National Monument
Las Cruces, NM
32.37248, -106.89744
paleontology | limited
The Robledo Mountains preserve an enormous concentration of fossil footprints and trackways from the Permian Period. Roads become high-clearance/4WD beyond the lower access, so travelers need to match the route to current vehicle conditions.
Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/prehistoric-trackways-national-monument

69. Kilbourne Hole Volcanic Crater
La Mesa, NM
31.97013, -106.95000
volcanic-geology | limited
This broad maar crater was blasted into the desert by explosive interaction between magma and groundwater. Remote dirt-road access and weather can make the last approach difficult, and collecting or off-road damage should never be encouraged.
Source: https://www.blm.gov/visit/kilbourne-hole-volcanic-crater

Radium Springs, NM
32.50550, -106.91835

Adobe ruins preserve a frontier fort where Buffalo Soldiers and other U.S. Army units served in the nineteenth century. The exposed walls and desert setting make the former post feel more skeletal than reconstructed military sites.

A large painted space mural marks a small museum devoted to NASA, rockets and aerospace memorabilia along U.S. 70. Its handmade roadside feel sets it apart from the larger institutional space museums elsewhere in the state.

32.26835, -107.75862

The former armory holds everything from Mimbres pottery and frontier objects to vehicles, dolls and military material. Its unusually broad, crowded collection makes it a classic small-town cabinet-of-curiosities museum.

73. Rockhound State Park
Deming, NM
