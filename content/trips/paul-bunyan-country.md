Paul Bunyan was never folklore in the way people assume. The stories circulated
in logging camps, certainly, but the version everyone knows was written down and
heavily embellished in the early twentieth century — some of it as advertising
copy for a timber company.

Northern Minnesota adopted him anyway, thoroughly and without apology, and this
route drives through the result.

## The order

West to east and then south, on two-lane roads through lake country. Two days is
comfortable; one is possible if you skip the museum.

You start at Fosston with a giant bluebird, which has nothing to do with Bunyan
and everything to do with the same impulse. Then Bemidji, which has the statue —
built in 1937, and one of the most photographed roadside figures in America.

Nevis, Akeley and Hackensack follow within forty minutes of each other, and this
is where it gets competitive. Akeley has a kneeling Paul with the museum behind
him. Hackensack has Lucette, presented as his sweetheart, which is an invention
laid on top of an invention and none the worse for it.

Pine River finishes with an 1887 railway caboose, a reminder of what actually
moved the timber.

## What is worth noticing

The statues are not all the same age or the same idea. Bemidji's is a
Depression-era civic project. The later ones are a small arms race between towns
on the same highway, each wanting the traffic.

That is the real story here, and it is the same one as Casey, Illinois: a
handful of small towns deciding that the way to survive is to build something
large enough that people stop.

## Practical

Most of these are free and outdoors. The museum in Akeley is volunteer-run and
generally open in summer only, so if it matters to you, ring ahead.

Winter changes this route considerably. The statues stand year round, but the
smaller places close and the driving is a different proposition.
