The tracks that produced the word *Bigfoot* were found at Bluff Creek in August
1958, on a logging road in the mountains east of here. There is nothing at the
site and the roads in are rough. This route goes to the places that grew up
around it instead.

## Klamath first

A fifty-foot Paul Bunyan and an equally large Babe stand at the entrance to
Trees of Mystery on US-101, and have since 1946. They are not Bigfoot, but they
are the same instinct in an older form: build something enormous beside a road
through the redwoods and people will pull over.

## Then east to Willow Creek

An hour and a half inland on SR-299, and the reason to make the trip.

Willow Creek decided in the 1960s that it was Bigfoot country and has never
wavered. The China Flat Museum keeps a serious Bigfoot collection — casts,
newspaper archive, material from the people who were actually there — and the
town carries several carved figures, including the Gonzalez statue.

The Bigfoot Daze festival runs every September if you want the town at full
volume.

Read the collection carefully and you will notice something the souvenir shops
do not advertise: the 1958 tracks were almost certainly made with carved wooden
feet, and Ray Wallace's family said so after he died in 2002. The museum does
not hide this, which is to its credit.

## Finish at Piercy

The world's tallest free-standing redwood chainsaw carving, on the Avenue of the
Giants end of the route. Chainsaw carving is the local art form up here for a
straightforward reason — there was a great deal of redwood and a great many
people with saws.

## Practical

SR-299 between Arcata and Willow Creek is a proper mountain road: slow, winding,
and no place to be in a hurry or in fog. Two days lets you drive it in daylight
both ways.

The museum keeps seasonal hours and is partly volunteer-run, so check before
building a day around it.
