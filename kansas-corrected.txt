OddWay — Kansas Complete
106 Kansas stops
Stops 1–106
Prepared September 10, 2026

Access key: open = normally accessible during posted/public hours; limited = seasonal, scheduled, ticketed, cemetery-hours, appointment-only, military/security-dependent, weather/road-dependent, private-access-dependent, or check-ahead access; roadside = view from a safe lawful public area; private = private property, do not enter without permission; seasonal = primarily accessible during a defined season; view-only = observe from a lawful public viewpoint and do not enter restricted infrastructure.

Research note: This list was built for OddWay around real, routable places with a specific unusual, haunted, folkloric, geological, roadside, dark-history, military, outsider-art, or specialized-museum reason to stop. Paranormal material is presented as reported lore rather than established fact. Access labels are intentionally conservative. First-party, government, museum, historical-organization, and official tourism sources were preferred; competitor/discovery sources are used only where a stronger primary source was not readily available. Coordinates are route pins rounded to five decimal places and are not permission to enter private, restricted, military, cemetery, or otherwise controlled land.

2. Garden of Eden
Lucas, KS
39.05790, -98.53515
outsider-art | open
Civil War veteran S.P. Dinsmoor transformed his home into a dense concrete sculpture environment filled with biblical scenes, political allegory, and handmade figures. His limestone cabin and unusual mausoleum make this one of the strongest outsider-art stops in the state.
Source: https://gardenofedenlucas.org/

3. Bowl Plaza
Lucas, KS
39.05898, -98.53836
roadside-oddity | open
Lucas turned a public restroom into a giant mosaic toilet-themed art environment, complete with sculptural plumbing references and colorful found-object details. It is functional civic infrastructure deliberately built as an absurd roadside attraction.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/listing/bowl-plaza/15640/

4. Grassroots Art Center
Lucas, KS
39.05756, -98.53832
outsider-art | open
This museum preserves Kansas folk, self-taught, and grassroots art made by people working outside conventional fine-art systems. The collection is especially useful as a gateway to the many eccentric art environments scattered across north-central Kansas.
Source: https://www.grassrootsart.net/

5. Florence Deeble Rock Garden
Lucas, KS
39.05852, -98.53642
outsider-art | open
Florence Deeble spent decades building miniature landscapes and travel memories from concrete, stone, and found material around her home. The result is a compact folk-art environment of mountains, villages, monuments, and personal geography.
Source: https://www.grassrootsart.net/

6. World's Largest Collection of the World's Smallest Versions of the World's Largest Things
Lucas, KS
39.05759, -98.53791
odd-museums | seasonal
Artist Erika Nelson created miniature versions of oversized roadside attractions from around the country, turning roadside gigantism back into tiny objects. The tongue-twisting collection functions as both museum and affectionate parody of American road-trip culture.
Source: https://www.worldslargestthings.com/

7. World's Largest Souvenir Travel Plate
Lucas, KS
39.05769, -98.52819
roadside-oddity | roadside
A giant roadside travel plate welcomes visitors on the edge of Lucas and celebrates the souvenir plates once collected on cross-country trips. Its exaggerated scale fits the town's larger tradition of deliberately strange public art.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/listing/worlds-largest-travel-plate/18782/

8. M.T. Liggett Art Environment
Mullinville, KS
37.58759, -99.48013
outsider-art | open
M.T. Liggett filled his roadside property with whirling metal sculptures, political caricatures, signs, and kinetic scrap-metal figures. The preserved environment remains one of Kansas's most confrontational and instantly recognizable folk-art landscapes.
Source: https://mtliggettartenvironment.org/

9. Big Brutus
West Mineral, KS
37.27348, -94.93820
industrial-history | open
This enormous electric mining shovel once stripped overburden from southeast Kansas coal fields and now stands preserved as a museum centerpiece. Its scale is difficult to understand until standing beneath the 16-story machine.
Source: https://www.bigbrutus.org/

10. Mushroom Rock State Park
Marquette, KS
38.72613, -98.02972
caves-geology | open
Wind and water eroded sandstone into oversized mushroom-shaped rocks balanced on narrow stems. The tiny state park is a concentrated collection of geological forms that look deliberately sculpted despite being natural.
Source: https://ksoutdoors.gov/about-kdwp/where-we-work/state-parks/mushroom-rock

11. Rock City
Minneapolis, KS
39.08750, -97.73167
caves-geology | open
More than 200 nearly spherical sandstone concretions cluster across a small landscape outside Minneapolis. Some are several meters across, creating a field of giant stone balls unlike most other Kansas geology.
Source: https://www.rockcitypark.com/

12. Monument Rocks
Oakley, KS
38.79326, -100.76268
caves-geology | limited
Towering chalk formations rise abruptly from the western Kansas plains and preserve evidence of the ancient inland sea that once covered the region. The formations are on private land opened to visitors, so access is a privilege and poor road conditions can close the route.
Source: https://www.visitoakleyks.com/monument-rocks

13. Little Jerusalem Badlands State Park
Oakley, KS
38.80281, -100.92820
caves-geology | open
White Niobrara chalk cliffs, spires, and narrow formations form a badlands landscape that looks dramatically different from the surrounding prairie. Visitors can use public overlooks and trails, while guided hikes reach more sensitive areas.
Source: https://ksoutdoors.gov/about-kdwp/where-we-work/state-parks/little-jerusalem

14. Castle Rock Badlands
Quinter, KS
38.86116, -100.16974
caves-geology | limited
Chalk spires and eroded badlands surround the isolated Castle Rock formation in western Kansas. The site is reached across private ranch roads, so visitors should only go in dry conditions and respect posted access rules.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/things-to-do/parks-and-nature/scenic-areas/castle-rock/

15. Arikaree Breaks
St. Francis, KS
39.91062, -101.78619
natural-oddities | roadside
Deep ravines and rugged loess formations cut through the far northwest corner of Kansas, producing terrain more often associated with neighboring states. Most land is private, but the breaks can be viewed from lawful public gravel roads.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/things-to-do/parks-and-nature/scenic-areas/arikaree-breaks/

16. Big Basin Prairie Preserve and St. Jacob's Well
Ashland, KS
37.24037, -99.98186
natural-oddities | limited
A broad natural sink and a smaller water-filled sinkhole known as St. Jacob's Well interrupt the prairie south of Dodge City. Local folklore and the unusual karst terrain make the preserve more than a normal scenic stop, but road and weather conditions can affect access.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

17. Geographic Center of the Contiguous United States
Lebanon, KS
39.82816, -98.56956
geographic-oddities | open
A stone monument and tiny chapel mark the traditional geographic center of the lower 48 states. The exact mathematical definition can vary, but this rural Kansas landmark became the enduring public symbol of the nation's middle.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

18. Mount Sunflower
Weskan, KS
39.02194, -102.03722
geographic-oddities | limited
Kansas's highest natural point is not a mountain but a subtle rise on private ranchland beside the Colorado line. The owners traditionally welcome respectful visitors, who find a sign, register, and whimsical summit markers rather than an alpine peak.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/listing/mount-sunflower/21305/

19. Kansas-Colorado-Oklahoma Tri-State Marker
Elkhart, KS
36.99323, -102.04204
geographic-oddities | roadside
A survey marker identifies the point where Kansas, Colorado, and Oklahoma meet. Reaching it requires rural roads near the edge of all three states, making the stop as much about standing on an abstract boundary as seeing a monument.
Source: https://www.ci.elkhart.ks.us/175/Tri-State-Marker

20. Four-State Lookout
White Cloud, KS
39.97975, -95.29953
geographic-oddities | open
A hilltop overlook above White Cloud is promoted for views across Kansas, Missouri, Nebraska, and, under clear conditions, toward Iowa. It is a simple public viewpoint whose appeal comes from the unusual multi-state geography.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

21. Giant Van Gogh 'Three Sunflowers in a Vase'
Goodland, KS
39.33794, -101.70482
roadside-oddity | open
A gigantic reproduction of Van Gogh's sunflower painting stands on an approximately 80-foot easel beside the highway. It was built as part of artist Cameron Cross's international Big Easel Project and has become Goodland's signature roadside landmark.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

22. High Plains Museum
Goodland, KS
39.34110, -101.70558
odd-museums | open
Goodland's city museum mixes regional artifacts with exhibits tied to inventions, transportation, and the unusual history of the High Plains. Its location only a short walk from the giant Van Gogh makes it a useful second OddWay stop rather than a generic local museum.
Source: https://www.cityofgoodland.org/

23. Fick Fossil and History Museum
Oakley, KS
39.12801, -100.86162
odd-museums | open
The Fick Museum combines western Kansas fossils with Vi Fick's eccentric folk art made from shark teeth, fish bones, shells, wax, and other materials. Large marine fossils are a reminder that this dry region once lay beneath the Western Interior Seaway.
Source: https://www.visitoakleyks.com/fick-fossil-history-museum

24. Buffalo Bill Cultural Center and Giant Bronze
Oakley, KS
39.12682, -100.86948
roadside-oddity | open
A twice-life-size bronze depicts Buffalo Bill Cody on horseback pursuing a buffalo near the place where local tradition says he earned his famous nickname in an 1868 hunting contest. The outdoor sculpture is accessible even when the visitor center is closed.
Source: https://www.visitoakleyks.com/buffalo-bill-cultural-center

25. Dorothy's House and Land of Oz
Liberal, KS
37.03436, -100.91041
odd-museums | open
Liberal built a replica of Dorothy Gale's Kansas home and an indoor Yellow Brick Road attraction around the state's connection to The Wizard of Oz. Guided visits lean heavily into handmade sets, memorabilia, and roadside-theater charm.
Source: https://visitliberal.com/

26. Mid-America Air Museum
Liberal, KS
37.03884, -100.95141
odd-museums | open
This city-operated museum holds one of the country's larger collections of historic aircraft, ranging from military machines to unusual civilian designs. The dense hangar presentation makes it a strong stop for aviation and mechanical-history travelers.
Source: https://liberalairmuseum.com/

27. International Pancake Day Hall of Fame
Liberal, KS
37.04132, -100.92400
odd-museums | limited
Liberal's long-running pancake race against Olney, England, has its own museum filled with racing memorabilia, frying pans, photographs, costumes, and international gifts. Outside the annual event it is generally visited by appointment.
Source: https://visitliberal.com/international-pancake-day/

28. World's Largest Czech Egg
Wilson, KS
38.82412, -98.47275
roadside-oddity | open
A 22-foot decorated kraslice stands in Wilson as a giant tribute to the community's Czech heritage. The colorful egg combines immigrant tradition with the oversized-object logic of a classic roadside attraction.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

29. Atlas Ad Astra Missile Silo
Wilson, KS
38.83378, -98.43086
military-history | limited
A decommissioned Atlas F intercontinental ballistic missile complex has been repurposed for tours, camping, and an evolving underground adventure project. Visitors can see Cold War infrastructure that was designed to launch a nuclear missile, but advance booking is required.
Source: https://www.atlasadastra.com/

30. Big Well Museum
Greensburg, KS
37.60341, -99.29444
industrial-history | open
The museum allows visitors to descend into the enormous hand-dug municipal well completed in 1888. Exhibits also document Greensburg's destruction in the 2007 tornado and the community's reconstruction.
Source: https://www.bigwell.org/

32. World's Largest Cow Hairball
Garden City, KS
37.96027, -100.87081
odd-museums | open
The Finney County Historical Museum displays a huge trichobezoar removed from a cow's stomach at a local packing plant. Once far heavier when wet, the dried hairball remains an unusually tactile museum object and one of Kansas's strangest documented artifacts.
Source: https://finneycounty.org/170/Historical-Museum

33. Sternberg Museum of Natural History
Hays, KS
38.89733, -99.31582
odd-museums | open
The Sternberg focuses on fossils and natural history from the central plains, including dramatic marine reptiles from the ancient inland sea. Its famous 'fish-within-a-fish' fossil and extensive Cretaceous collection give it a strong prehistoric OddWay hook.
Source: https://sternberg.fhsu.edu/

34. Fort Hays State Historic Site
Hays, KS
38.86198, -99.34314
frontier-history | open
Four surviving buildings preserve part of the frontier military post associated with Buffalo Soldiers, railroad expansion, and figures such as Custer, Hickok, and Cody. The open landscape makes the fort's isolation and military role easier to understand than a conventional indoor museum.
Source: https://www.kansashistory.gov/fort_hays

35. Strataca
Hutchinson, KS
38.04370, -97.86755
industrial-history | open
Visitors descend about 650 feet into a working-area salt formation more than 275 million years old. Underground galleries combine mining history, geology, preserved film materials, and rides through former mine passages.
Source: https://underkansas.org/

36. Cosmosphere
Hutchinson, KS
38.06561, -97.92138
space-history | open
The Cosmosphere houses major American and Soviet spaceflight artifacts, including flown spacecraft and Cold War-era hardware. Its concentration of genuine space objects makes it far more than a general science museum for OddWay purposes.
Source: https://cosmo.org/

37. Little House on the Prairie Museum
Independence, KS
37.12487, -95.83678
literary-history | seasonal
The museum complex stands near the Kansas homestead associated with Laura Ingalls Wilder's family and includes a reconstructed cabin and period buildings. It ties a globally known frontier story to a specific rural Kansas landscape.
Source: https://littlehouseontheprairiemuseum.com/

38. Norman No. 1 Oil Well
Neodesha, KS
37.41250, -95.62778
industrial-history | open
A reconstructed wooden derrick marks the site of the 1892 Norman No. 1, widely recognized as the first commercially successful oil well in the Mid-Continent field. The small site represents the beginning of an industry that transformed Kansas and neighboring states.
Source: https://www.kansashistory.gov/

39. Dalton Gang Hideout and Escape Tunnel
Meade, KS
37.28096, -100.33710
outlaw-history | open
A house linked to relatives of the Dalton brothers became famous for a concealed tunnel running from the home toward a barn. Whether every outlaw story attached to it is literal or embellished, the preserved tunnel and Dalton connection make it an unusually physical piece of western folklore.
Source: http://www.oldmeadecounty.com/hideout.htm

40. Stockade Museum
Medicine Lodge, KS
37.27562, -98.58168
frontier-history | open
A reconstructed stockade and museum interpret Medicine Lodge's frontier period, regional conflict, settlement, and treaty-era history. The enclosed timber structure gives the site a distinctive physical character compared with a standard county museum.
Source: https://www.medicinelodgestockade.org/

41. Carry Nation Home
Medicine Lodge, KS
37.27555, -98.58179
weird-history | open
The preserved home of temperance activist Carry A. Nation sits beside the Stockade Museum complex. Nation became nationally famous for smashing saloons with a hatchet, giving the modest house an unusually vivid connection to American protest history.
Source: https://www.medicinelodgestockade.org/

42. Boyer Museum of Animated Carvings
Belleville, KS
39.82867, -97.63075
outsider-art | seasonal
Paul Boyer's hand-built wooden automata move through elaborate mechanical scenes powered by gears, cams, wire, and motors. The museum preserves decades of one-person engineering and folk art rather than mass-produced displays.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

43. Pony Express Barn Museum
Marysville, KS
39.84166, -96.64802
transportation-history | open
An original 1859 stone barn served as a Pony Express home station where riders and horses could be changed. The museum preserves one of the few substantial surviving structures directly connected to the short-lived mail service.
Source: https://www.visitmarysvilleks.org/

44. Hollenberg Pony Express Station
Hanover, KS
39.90091, -96.84381
transportation-history | seasonal
Built in the late 1850s, Hollenberg Station served emigrant trails and later the Pony Express without being moved from its original site. Its survival makes it one of Kansas's strongest physical links to overland migration and the famous 18-month mail route.
Source: https://www.kansashistory.gov/hollenberg

45. Pawnee Indian Museum State Historic Site
Republic, KS
39.90785, -97.86100
indigenous-history | seasonal
The museum protects the excavated floor of a large Pawnee earth lodge from a village occupied in the late 1700s and early 1800s. Archaeology, a rare sacred bundle, and visible lodge depressions make the landscape itself part of the interpretation.
Source: https://www.kansashistory.gov/pawnee_indian

46. Kansas Barbed Wire Museum
La Crosse, KS
38.52468, -99.31162
odd-museums | seasonal
Thousands of varieties of barbed wire and fencing tools document how many different ways inventors tried to control the open plains. A subject that sounds mundane becomes a surprisingly dense museum of patents, violence, ranching, and material culture.
Source: https://barbedwiremuseum.com/

47. They Also Ran Gallery
Norton, KS
39.82940, -99.88927
odd-museums | limited
This unusual political gallery focuses not on presidents but on major-party presidential nominees who lost. Portraits and biographies turn American history toward the people who came close to the White House and then largely disappeared from popular memory.
Source: http://www.theyalsoran.com/

48. Moline Swinging Bridge
Moline, KS
37.36578, -96.30433
architectural-oddities | open
A narrow pedestrian suspension bridge crosses Wildcat Creek and is locally described as Kansas's oldest swinging bridge. Its movement underfoot and small-town park setting give it the kind of simple physical oddity that works well as a route stop.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

49. OZ Museum
Wamego, KS
39.20122, -96.30530
odd-museums | open
Wamego's museum gathers Wizard of Oz books, film material, replicas, collectibles, and themed environments into a dedicated Oz attraction. Kansas's fictional role in the story is turned into a substantial real-world roadside destination.
Source: https://ozmuseum.com/

50. Original Pizza Hut Museum
Wichita, KS
37.72103, -97.28654
odd-museums | open
The tiny original Pizza Hut building was moved onto Wichita State University's campus and turned into a museum about the global restaurant chain that began there in 1958. It is an unusually small birthplace for an enormous commercial brand.
Source: https://www.wichita.edu/about/wsunews/pizza_hut_museum.php

51. Museum of World Treasures
Wichita, KS
37.68705, -97.32789
odd-museums | open
This eclectic museum deliberately mixes fossils, ancient objects, military history, mummies, and other artifacts rather than focusing on a single period. The breadth and cabinet-of-curiosities feel make it a stronger OddWay fit than a conventional history museum.
Source: https://worldtreasures.org/

52. Kansas Aviation Museum
Wichita, KS
37.63217, -97.27372
transportation-history | open
The museum occupies Wichita's former Art Deco municipal airport terminal and surrounds it with aircraft tied to the city's aviation industry. Visitors can explore both the historic terminal architecture and a collection of unusual airframes.
Source: https://kansasaviationmuseum.org/

53. Keeper of the Plains
Wichita, KS
37.69138, -97.34968
indigenous-history | open
Blackbear Bosin's 44-foot steel figure stands at the confluence of the Arkansas and Little Arkansas rivers. The surrounding plaza, footbridges, and scheduled fire pots make the monument one of Kansas's most distinctive large-scale public artworks.
Source: https://www.wichita.gov/

54. Old Cowtown Museum
Wichita, KS
37.69379, -97.36225
living-history | open
A collection of historic and reconstructed buildings creates a walkable 19th-century townscape covering frontier and early Wichita life. Its saloons, businesses, streets, and demonstrations offer a more immersive old-west environment than a display-case museum.
Source: https://www.oldcowtown.org/

55. Combat Air Museum
Topeka, KS
38.94671, -95.68446
military-history | open
Two hangars at Forbes Field hold dozens of military aircraft, helicopters, engines, missiles, and aviation artifacts. The volunteer-run collection spans early flight through Cold War jets and gives visitors unusually close access to large machines.
Source: https://combatairmuseum.org/

56. Brown v. Board of Education National Historical Park
Topeka, KS
39.03788, -95.67614
dark-history | open
The National Park Service preserves the former Monroe Elementary School, one of the segregated schools directly connected to the landmark Supreme Court case. The classrooms and exhibits make the legal history tangible at the place where part of it happened.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/brvb/

57. Rochester Cemetery — 'Albino Lady' Folklore
Topeka, KS
39.10435, -95.67988
folklore | limited
Rochester Cemetery is attached to a long-running Topeka legend about an 'Albino Lady' said to appear among the graves. Treat the story as local folklore rather than fact, visit only during lawful cemetery hours, and do not disturb graves or residents nearby.
Source: https://www.visittopeka.com/

58. Sallie House
Atchison, KS
39.56659, -95.11495
haunted | limited
The 19th-century house is the center of Atchison's best-known ghost story, commonly associated with a child called Sallie and decades of paranormal reports. It operates as a bookable attraction, so entry should only be through official tours or reservations.
Source: https://visitatchison.com/highlight/sallie-house

59. McInteer Villa
Atchison, KS
39.56334, -95.13351
haunted | limited
This imposing 1889 brick mansion is both a historic home and one of Atchison's established paranormal-tour locations. Stories of apparitions and unexplained activity are part of the property's visitor experience, but should be understood as reported lore rather than verified events.
Source: https://visitatchison.com/

60. Amelia Earhart Birthplace Museum
Atchison, KS
39.56375, -95.11448
aviation-history | open
The Gothic Revival house overlooking the Missouri River is the birthplace of pioneering aviator Amelia Earhart. Period rooms and personal material tie one of aviation's most enduring mysteries to a very specific Kansas home.
Source: https://www.ameliaearhartmuseum.org/

61. Amelia Earhart Hangar Museum
Atchison, KS
39.56846, -95.17826
aviation-history | open
A modern museum at Amelia Earhart Airport centers on Muriel, the world's last remaining Lockheed Electra 10-E of the same model Earhart flew on her final journey. Interactive exhibits explore her life, aircraft, navigation, and disappearance.
Source: https://ameliaearharthangarmuseum.org/

62. Mount Vernon Cemetery
Atchison, KS
39.54160, -95.14250
cemetery-history | limited
This historic cemetery contains prominent Atchison burials and sits near the giant Amelia Earhart earthwork portrait visible from above. It is a legitimate historic destination, but visitors should follow cemetery hours and treat gravesites respectfully.
Source: https://visitatchison.com/

63. Moon Marble Company
Bonner Springs, KS
39.05921, -94.87690
odd-museums | open
Moon Marble combines a toy store with live demonstrations of traditional handmade glass marbles and an enormous assortment of unusual games and toys. Watching molten glass become a finished marble gives the stop a craft-process hook beyond normal retail.
Source: https://moonmarble.com/

64. National Agricultural Center and Hall of Fame
Bonner Springs, KS
39.10817, -94.87368
odd-museums | seasonal
A large museum campus preserves historic farm machinery, rural buildings, agricultural technology, and the National Agricultural Hall of Fame. The scale and variety of obsolete machines make it a useful stop for industrial and mechanical oddities.
Source: https://aghalloffame.com/

65. Baxter Springs Heritage Center and Museum
Baxter Springs, KS
37.02804, -94.73335
weird-history | open
Baxter Springs combines Route 66, mining, Civil War, and frontier history in a town that repeatedly sat on important transportation routes. The museum is a strong context stop before visiting the town's surviving Route 66 structures.
Source: https://baxterspringsmuseum.org/

66. Baxter Springs 1930 Phillips 66 Station
Baxter Springs, KS
37.02593, -94.73480
route-66 | open
A restored cottage-style Phillips 66 station now serves as a Route 66 visitor center. The tiny station preserves the look of early automobile travel on the Kansas segment of the Mother Road.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/places/phillips-66-service-station-baxter-springs.htm

67. Rainbow Bridge
Riverton, KS
37.07352, -94.74083
route-66 | roadside
This concrete Marsh arch bridge carried old Route 66 over Brush Creek and is the last surviving bridge of its type on the historic highway in Kansas. Its compact scale and distinctive white arches make it an easy roadside architecture stop.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

68. Old Riverton Store
Riverton, KS
37.07494, -94.70279
route-66 | open
The 1925 general store has served generations of travelers and remains one of the most authentic operating businesses on Kansas Route 66. Its groceries, deli, old building fabric, and road history give it more substance than a recreated nostalgia stop.
Source: https://www.eislerbros.com/

69. Cars on the Route
Galena, KS
37.08037, -94.63890
route-66 | open
A restored Kan-O-Tex service station displays the rusty boom truck that helped inspire Tow Mater during research for Pixar's Cars. The station links real Route 66 history with a modern pop-culture version of the road.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

70. Gearhead Curios
Galena, KS
37.07553, -94.63930
route-66 | open
A restored 1939 filling station has been turned into a Route 66 curiosity stop filled with automotive memorabilia, art, and roadside installations. Its giant gearhead figure and preserved station architecture make it a distinct Galena stop from Cars on the Route.
Source: https://gearheadcurios.com/

71. Brown Mansion
Coffeyville, KS
37.01995, -95.61445
architectural-oddities | seasonal
Oil businessman W.P. Brown built this elaborate early-1900s mansion with ornate interiors and unusually advanced systems for its era. Local ghost stories have accumulated around the property, but its documented architecture and history are the primary reasons to visit.
Source: https://www.coffeyville.com/

72. National Orphan Train Complex
Concordia, KS
39.57461, -97.66150
dark-history | open
This museum documents the orphan-train movement that relocated tens of thousands of children from eastern cities to communities across the country. A restored Union Pacific depot and personal stories make a difficult, often complicated social history concrete.
Source: https://orphantraindepot.org/

73. Camp Concordia POW Camp Site
Concordia, KS
39.61167, -97.64028
military-history | limited
The remains and museum work at Camp Concordia preserve the site of a World War II prisoner-of-war camp that held thousands of German soldiers. Surviving structures and markers sit on the former camp landscape north of town; check current tour and access information before visiting.
Source: https://www.travelks.com/

74. Boot Hill Museum
Dodge City, KS
37.75351, -100.02218
old-west | open
Boot Hill interprets Dodge City's cattle-town and gunfighter era with historic objects, reconstructed streetscapes, and the mythology that grew around the town. It deliberately occupies the line between documented frontier history and the larger-than-life western image.
Source: https://boothill.org/

75. Santa Fe Trail Ruts
Dodge City, KS
37.79194, -100.19694
historic-trails | open
Deep parallel depressions west of Dodge City preserve wagon traffic from the Santa Fe Trail in the actual prairie surface. Seeing the route physically worn into the ground is more immediate than a marker describing where a trail once passed.
Source: https://www.nps.gov/

76. Mueller-Schmidt House — Home of Stone
Dodge City, KS
37.75551, -100.01554
architectural-oddities | limited
Built from native limestone in the 1880s, the Mueller-Schmidt House survives as one of Dodge City's most substantial early residences. Its preserved rooms offer a quieter, more authentic counterpoint to the town's saloon-and-gunfighter mythology.
Source: https://www.visitdodgecity.org/

77. Ellinwood Underground Tunnels
Ellinwood, KS
38.35377, -98.58082
underground-history | limited
Passages beneath downtown Ellinwood preserve remnants of underground storefronts, storage spaces, bath facilities, and later Prohibition-era stories. Tours are scheduled rather than freely accessible, and some sections remain beneath active private buildings.
Source: https://www.ellinwoodchamber.com/ellinwood-underground-tunnels

78. Historic Wolf Hotel
Ellinwood, KS
38.35377, -98.58082
haunted | limited
The restored 1894 Wolf Hotel is a working lodging and event property connected with Ellinwood's underground tunnel network. It also markets paranormal and history experiences, so ghost claims should be treated as part of the property's lore rather than established fact.
Source: https://historicwolfhotel.com/

79. M65 Atomic Cannon — Junction City
