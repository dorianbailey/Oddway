Three days across the emptiest and hottest country in the index. This is the
route where preparation is not optional.

## Out of Las Vegas

Start with the Haunted Museum on Charleston Boulevard, which is the last
air-conditioned thing you will see for a while, then head northwest.

**Rhyolite** is the easy one: BLM-managed, walk freely among the ruins of a town
that appeared in the Bullfrog gold rush and emptied within a decade.
**Leadfield** is the better story — a boom that lasted under a year in 1926,
driven by promotion rather than ore. It sits on the one-way Titus Canyon Road,
**closing October 2026 to September 2027 for repairs**, so check first.

## The hard middle

**Ballarat** and **Cerro Gordo** are both privately owned and neither is a
visitor attraction in the way the word usually means.

Ballarat is adobe ruins off Panamint Valley Road with no dependable services.
Cerro Gordo is eight miles up a steep dirt road into the Inyo Mountains, and the
whole townsite belongs to somebody — follow the owner's current rules rather
than treating it as open ground.

Neither is a casual detour. Both are worth it if you go equipped.

## Back to the tarmac

**Baker** and the world's tallest thermometer, 134 feet for the 134 degrees
recorded at Furnace Creek in 1913. Then **Calico**, restored by Walter Knott and
handed to San Bernardino County in 1966 — ticketed, busy, and after four days of
desert, pleasantly ordinary.

## Take this seriously

Summer here is genuinely dangerous. Carry far more water than you think, fuel at
every opportunity, and tell somebody your route. Phone signal is absent for long
stretches and the unpaved sections want proper clearance.

Spring and autumn are the seasons for this. July is not.
