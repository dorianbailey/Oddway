This is the only trip in the index you can do on foot.

Casey sits on I-70 halfway between St Louis and Indianapolis, and everything
here is within a few blocks of East Main Street. The whole thing is free and
outdoors and open all year.

## Start with the wind chime

Not because it is the biggest — the rocking chair across the street is more
imposing — but because it is the one that started it. Jim Bolin built it in
2011 out of recycled pipeline from his own company, to draw people off the
interstate and into a tea shop he was not sure the town could support.

It worked, and he spent the next fifteen years doing it again.

## The order barely matters

The listed order walks you from the wind chime and the rocking chair on East
Main, over to the teeter totter, then the mailbox, and out to the smaller
pieces. Any order works. The town is 2.6 square miles.

What matters more is not rushing. It is possible to photograph all seven in
twenty minutes and come away thinking you have seen a novelty. The interesting
thing is not any single object — it is that a town of 2,300 has thirty of them,
built on purpose, by one person, because he wanted the shops to stay open.

## Two things worth knowing

**They are meant to be used.** A Guinness record requires the object to perform
its function, so the chime chimes, the chair rocks, and the mailbox takes post.
Sit on things.

**There are more than seven.** OddWay lists the ones mapped in OpenStreetMap;
there are over thirty around town, including a pitchfork at a restaurant and
softball bats at the hall of fame. The town publishes a proper map, and it is
worth picking one up.

## And

Jim Bolin died in an accident in January 2026. The objects are all still there
and the town still runs on them, but it is worth knowing whose work you are
looking at.
