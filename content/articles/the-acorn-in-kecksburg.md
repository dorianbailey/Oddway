On the evening of 9 December 1965, a fireball crossed the sky over the American
Midwest and the eastern states. Thousands of people saw it. It was reported from
Michigan down through Ohio and into Pennsylvania, and it ended somewhere near
the village of Kecksburg, in Westmoreland County.

Astronomers had a straightforward answer at the time and have not changed it: a
meteor, or possibly a piece of re-entering spacecraft debris. Something came in
fast and burned.

## What Kecksburg says happened next

That residents saw something come down in the woods. That it was not a rock but
an object — acorn-shaped, the size of a small car, with markings around the
base that some described as resembling hieroglyphics.

That the military arrived quickly, sealed the area, removed something on a
flatbed truck, and left. That people who went to look were turned back.

The Air Force position was that nothing was found. NASA said later it had no
record of recovering anything. In the 2000s a journalist sued NASA under freedom
of information law for records relating to the night; the case dragged on for
years and produced little.

That is the whole of it. A bright object with a mundane explanation, a
community that remembers an unusual amount of official activity, and no
documentation either way.

## The acorn by the fire station

Behind the Kecksburg Volunteer Fire Department there is a large metal acorn on a
pole, with the hieroglyph-like band around its base, exactly as described.

It was built as a prop.

*Unsolved Mysteries* filmed a segment about the incident in 1990 and had the
object made for the reconstruction. When the crew finished, the village kept it,
mounted it, and it has been there ever since.

This is not a secret and nobody in Kecksburg pretends otherwise. It is a replica
of a description, built for television, adopted by the place the description
came from.

## Why that is the interesting part

Most towns with a story like this eventually build something. What is unusual
here is the honesty of the object: it is not a monument to a proven event, it is
a monument to *what people said they saw*, and its provenance makes that
explicit.

The village runs a UFO festival each summer. The fire department benefits. A
volunteer service in a small Pennsylvania township is funded partly by a
television prop of a thing that was probably a meteor, and there is something
genuinely good-natured about that arrangement.

## Going there

The acorn is outside the fire station on Water Street and can be seen at any
time from the road. It is an active volunteer fire station, so park sensibly
and keep the apron clear.
