Most OddWay routes are about the driving. This one is about parking once.

Pioche sits at six thousand feet in eastern Nevada with about a thousand
residents, and nearly everything worth seeing is on one street or visible from
it. Silver was found here in the 1860s; by the early 1870s it was one of the
roughest camps in the West, and the buildings that survive were all put up by
people who expected the money to last.

## Two theatres sharing a wall

Start at the **Overland**, which is a working hotel and saloon and the easiest
place to leave a car.

A minute north are **Thompson's Opera House** and the **Gem Theater**, and
these two are worth slowing down for. They are separate buildings, put up
sixty years apart, standing shoulder to shoulder at 644 and 648 Main. The
wooden one went up in 1873 as Brown's Hall. In the 1930s it was renamed the
Gem. Then in 1937 somebody built a purpose-made brick cinema on the plot next
door, took the name Gem with it, and the older building went back to being
Thompson's.

So the name has been on both buildings, at different times, and every list of
Pioche's historic structures has to say which one it means. The opera house
opens by arrangement; the cinema has been closed since 2002 and is worth
looking at from the pavement.

## The courthouse that ate the county

The **Million Dollar Courthouse** is the story everybody tells about Pioche and
it is true. The 1872 building was meant to cost around twenty-six thousand
dollars. Refinanced, re-refinanced, and compounded by a county that kept
putting the problem off, the debt reached nearly a million by the time it was
finally cleared — in 1938, by which point the building had been condemned and
abandoned for six years.

## Up the hill

The **Lincoln County Historical Museum** collects what the scattered ghost
towns of the county left behind. Above it, the **aerial tramway** still crosses
the hillside with ore buckets hanging from the cables, which is not a
reconstruction and not a monument — it is just still there.

## Boot Hill

The last stretch is the steepest, and the point of the walk.

**Boot Hill** holds the men who died in Pioche's first years. The town's claim
is that seventy-two people were buried here before anybody died of natural
causes. Like most such claims it has been repeated more often than it has been
checked, but the graves are real and the markers are plain, and the difference
between reading that and standing in it is the reason to come.

## Practical

About a mile end to end, uphill on the return, at six thousand feet. Allow an
afternoon rather than the twenty minutes of actual walking — the museum and the
courthouse are where the time goes.

The Overland and the courthouse keep regular hours. Thompson's opens by
arrangement rather than on a schedule, so ring ahead if getting inside matters
to you. The tramway and the Gem are outdoor looking, any time.
