The Upper Peninsula is three hundred miles wide, has about three per cent of
Michigan's population, and is culturally closer to northern Wisconsin than to
Detroit. People here call themselves Yoopers and everyone below the bridge a
troll, because trolls live under bridges.

This is the length of it, east to west, and four days is not generous.

## Over the bridge

**St Ignace** first, and the Mystery Spot — a tilted shack of the kind this
index has a piece about. Every one of them is the same room, and knowing that
does not stop it working.

Then north to **Paradise** for the Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum at Whitefish
Point. This is the serious stop of the route. Whitefish Bay has taken more ships
than anywhere else on the lakes, and the museum holds the bell from the *Edmund
Fitzgerald*, raised in 1995 at the request of the families. Twenty-nine men are
still down there.

## The middle

**Grand Marais** has the Pickle Barrel House, a two-storey barrel built in 1926
as a summer cottage for the cartoonist who drew the Teenie Weenies.

**Lakenenland** near Marquette is a sculpture park a welder built beside the
highway from scrap and left open, free, all year, with a donation box. Drive
through it slowly.

**Da Yoopers Tourist Trap** at Ishpeming is the U.P. explaining itself to
visitors on its own terms, and is funnier than that makes it sound.

## Copper country

The Keweenaw Peninsula produced more copper than anywhere in the world for a
time, and the industry is what built and then emptied these towns.

**Quincy Mine** takes you down a shaft that goes over nine thousand feet.

**Italian Hall** in Calumet is not a monument in the ordinary sense. During the
1913 copper strike, someone shouted "fire" into a crowded Christmas party for
strikers' children. There was no fire. Seventy-three people died in the
stairwell, most of them children. The building was demolished in 1984 and only
the sandstone doorway they died trying to get through is left standing, in a
small park. Go, and be quiet about it.

## The last stretch

**The Paulding Light** appears down a power line corridor near Watersmeet most
nights. Michigan Tech students identified it in 2010 as headlights on US-45
about four and a half miles away, visible through a gap in the terrain. It is
still worth the drive, and the car park still fills up.

**Ironwood** finishes the route with a giant Stormy Kromer cap, which is a hat
made in Wisconsin and worn devoutly here.

## Practical

Four days minimum. Distances are long, towns are far apart, and phone signal is
patchy through the middle.

Most of this is summer-only. Several stops close entirely between October and
May, and the U.P. gets two hundred inches of snow — winter is a completely
different trip requiring a completely different vehicle.
